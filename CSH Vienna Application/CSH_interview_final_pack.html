4. What you told CSH (keep answers consistent)

  • Q1, your project: dissertation on UK economic policy reaction, 255,446 comments from X, Reddit, YouTube and Quora, ethics approval, BERT against BiLSTM and VADER, calibration, McNemar, bootstrap, Prophet, NRC emotions, LIME. What went wrong: BERT’s macro-F1 of 0.878 was scored against VADER-seeded labels and the 200-post check reviewed VADER’s labels, so validation was circular; and you had assumed “negative” means the same on every platform. Lesson: an agreement score describes the reference as much as the model.
  • Q2, CSH discovery: Chen, Kmetty, Iñiguez and Omodei (2025, Communication Methods and Measures): linking a Hungarian survey to donated YouTube histories shows commenters are more polarised and selective than watchers, so comment-based studies overstate division. Your question: is the commenter–watcher gap stable enough that a small donated panel could calibrate polarisation estimates from public comments at scale?
  • Q3, your problem: when is a machine-labelled construct (sentiment, stance, frame) comparable across platforms, annotator models and time, and how to correct it before tracking narrative spread. Tractable because (1) the simulation runs in seconds and can add models and time as facets, (2) you can build a corpus around events fixed in advance with a hand-labelled sample per platform and a second coder, (3) concept erasure tests whether removing the platform signal removes real differences, (4) corrected and uncorrected measures go into a network diffusion analysis. Closest to Alexander Hoyle’s work; between the narratives-at-scale and polarisation springboard projects. Gap: network science of diffusion.

6. Your paper in 20 seconds

“AI reads opinions differently on Reddit than on YouTube, because people write differently on each. So adding platforms together can flip the answer: in my simulation, the overall mood points the wrong way on nearly a quarter of days, and under one day in sixty once each platform is corrected. The paper gives a checklist to test whether a comparison is fair, and a way to fix it.”

7. Practice answers (45–75 seconds each)

Tell us about yourself.

I’m a data engineer and researcher from Pakistan, and I work on social media data. I started web scraping on Fiverr as a computer science student, did more than 300 projects, then built Dubizzle Labs’ first scraping system across 15 markets in four languages and was promoted twice. A Chevening Scholarship took me to Portsmouth for an MSc in Data Analytics, where my dissertation compared public reaction to UK economic policy across four platforms. Today I work on social media analysis at M+C Saatchi Fluency in London, for brands and UK government, and I’m co-founder and CTO of Artemis AI. Last week my first paper, with my supervisor, went live on arXiv.

Describe a research project and what went wrong.

Use section 4, Q1: the project, the circular validation you found yourself, the paper it became, the quarter-of-days result, and the lesson about independent yardsticks.

What have you built in industry, and what did it teach you?

Use section 3: one Fluency example (prompt-versioned LLM tagging), one Artemis AI example (explained labels, dialect and banter filters), then the one-sentence thread.

Why a PhD, and why now?

Industry taught me to collect and label online data at scale; it didn’t teach me to show rigorously what those numbers say about people. Publishing the paper showed me this is the work I enjoy most, and I want the training to do it properly, then carry it back into how these tools are built.

Why CSH, and why complexity science?

Opinion online is a system, not a pile of posts: people, platforms and algorithms shape each other, and what we can see is a selected slice. Chen and colleagues showed commenters are more polarised than watchers, which is my problem in another form. Alexander Hoyle’s validation work is closest to mine, and I want to learn network science and diffusion, which I don’t have yet.

What would you work on?

Use section 4, Q3, in three steps: extend the simulation, build an event-anchored corpus labelled by several LLMs with human checks, then show when diffusion conclusions change after correction.

Strengths and gaps?

Use section 5: name two strengths with evidence, then the two gaps and how you will close them.

Teamwork, a setback, five years?

Team: led three at Dubizzle and three at VendueTech, lead the technology in a team of eight at Artemis AI. Setback: the circular validation. Five years: researching how online opinion is measured, so the numbers people act on are tested against people.

Questions for us? (one only)

How are students matched with supervisors in the first year, and is co-supervision across groups common?
