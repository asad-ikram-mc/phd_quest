MSc dissertation (Portsmouth 2025, Dr Haig)

  • Q: how did UK reaction to economic policy differ across X, Reddit, YouTube, Quora?
  • Did: ethics, 255,446 comments (official APIs + licensed feeds), VADER, BiLSTM, fine-tuned BERT (macro-F1 0.878), McNemar, bootstrap, Prophet, NRC, LIME.
  • Found: mood dropped at the 2022 mini-budget; YouTube calmer, X angrier.
  • What went wrong: BERT was trained on VADER labels and checked against them; my 200-post check (κ≈0.78) reviewed VADER, so it was circular. And I assumed “negative” means the same on every platform.
  • Lesson: the yardstick must be independent; groups need an invariance test first.

Q2 answer: CSH discovery (Omodei group)

Chen, Kmetty, Iñiguez & Omodei (2025): Hungarian survey + donated YouTube histories show commenters are more polarized than watchers, so comment-based studies overstate division.
My question: is the commenter–watcher gap stable across platforms, topics, time, so a small donated panel could correct polarization estimates at scale? (Same logic as score linking.)

Paper: “Is the Platform Part of the Measurement?” (arXiv, Oct 2026, with Dr Haig)

Idea: each platform is like a different survey mode. Check before you compare.
1 Same moment: 15 anchor events, fixed in advance→2 Check vs people: G-study→3 Same meaning? invariance + DIF→4 Who posts: multilevel model→5 One yardstick: score linking→6 Show the cost: simulation
Hook: same time, check, meaning, crowd, convert, prove. (Exam marks from 4 schools; the AI is the marker.)
  • Simulation (Python, 500 runs, known true mood): naive pooling gets the sign wrong on 13.8 of 60 days (~a quarter); adjusted 0.8.
  • Every test repeated with a second model (RoBERTa).
  • Next: gold sample 1,000–1,500 posts + a second annotator. Real data is the next study.

“You used VADER…?”

“Yes, and that’s why the paper exists. In my MSc BERT was judged against VADER, so it was circular. In the paper people are the yardstick, RoBERTa double-checks, and events are fixed in advance.”
