5. Your paper in depth“Is the Platform Part of the Measurement? A Protocol and Simulation Study for Cross-Platform Equivalence of Machine-Labelled Policy Sentiment” · Ikram & Haig · arXiv 2610.16757 · cs.LG, cross-listed cs.CL, cs.CY, cs.SI · code: github.com/asadfix/platform-measurement

The problem, as a picture

Why: the gap

Studies compare AI scores across platforms as if a score means the same everywhere. Sen et al. (2021) named this platform-affordance error (a platform's features, like character limits or threading, change how opinion is written and scored) but gave no test. Survey research solved the same problem for “mode effects” (phone vs paper). Core claim: a platform is a mode.

When: the timeline

  • 2025: MSc dissertation (the motivating case).
  • 21 Aug 2026: 15 anchor events fixed in advance.
  • Sep 2026: draft with Dr Haig, reviewed.
  • 30 Sep: Dr Haig approves posting.
  • 10 Oct: endorsed for arXiv by Dr Rahim Taheri.
  • 12 Oct: v1.1 after an 8-angle review.
  • 14 Oct 2026: live on arXiv.

How: the six steps (click through)

The simulation result (500 repeats, 60 days each)

Just add platforms
13.8 days
Correct each first
0.8 days

Days when the overall mood pointed the wrong way, out of 60. How it was built: a known true mood with a shock at an event; each platform sees it through its own bias, noise and thresholds. Sensitivity: the bigger the difference between platforms, the worse it gets (0.8 → 1.8 → 4.5 → 13.8 → 42.9 wrong days). Honest caveat: the corrected index used the true platform settings; real estimates will be noisier.

Safeguard: two-classifier check

Every test is repeated with a second, non-word-list AI model. A platform difference only counts if both models show it, so it isn't one tool's quirk.

What's new vs earlier work

Earlier studies treated annotators (Kennedy 2020; Sachdeva 2022), prompts and models (Camuffo 2026), survey answers (Boyd 2024) or survey vs traces (Cernat 2025) as the source of difference. Yours treats the platform itself as the source, for AI-labelled text.

Five honest limits

  1. Who posts mixes with how the platform shapes posts.
  2. Four platforms; maybe fewer after the data check.
  3. Measures expressed feeling, not policy support.
  4. UK and English only.
  5. Some dissertation figures are inconsistent (disclosed).

What comes next

Fresh data collection; 1,000–1,500 comments labelled by people, blind to the AI, sampled by platform, event and class; a second annotator on a quarter to a third; then all six steps on real data. In the PhD: add AI models, time and how narratives spread.
Your paper in 20 seconds“People write differently on Reddit than on YouTube, so AI reads their opinions differently. Adding platforms together can flip the answer: in my simulation the overall mood pointed the wrong way on nearly a quarter of days. My paper gives a checklist to test whether a comparison is fair, and a way to fix it.”

7. Artemis AI in depthCo-founder & CTO · social media intelligence for creators and businesses · team of eight · beta 15 Dec 2026 · pilots 1 Dec

The system, end to end

The 48 models, by group (tap a group)

Status today: 2 of 35 product models finished, 11 in progress; the rest planned. Say “48 models in the agreed architecture, being built for the beta”.

The labelling chain

Five tasks on every post: sentiment, emotion, topic, intent, toxicity. Claude labels cost about $10 for 6,000; DeepSeek about $2.50 for 12,000. Next: a blind, stratified human gold set with a second labeller.

Network theory: built, planned and vision

Built (early)

A first page network from warehouse data only (topics, content types, image fingerprints, formats, posting times), for consenting pilot pages, with no personal data. A page-to-page affinity graph links pages whose audiences comment alike; a prototype runs Louvain community detection (finds clusters in a graph) and a 0–100 match score.

Planned

Audience communities in the page graph; a collaboration match score rated on 100 page pairs; “new-group reach” (which partner opens a new audience); a collab outcome estimator (did a collab lift both pages over 30 days?); opt-in weekly Discovery; a rising-topics tool tested against a seasonal baseline, with groups under 5 pages hidden.

Vision (strategy, not built)

Your CEO's ideas: identity gravity (creators whose content mix drifts together may suit a collab), a signal economy (different weights for likes, comments, shares, collabs) and diffusion paths (ideas spreading cluster → bridge → cluster), always as probabilities.
Two research catches you can mention“Our Discovery plan assumed Meta gives commenter identity; it doesn't, so we rebuilt it on page-level signals only. And we had to define a good match before scoring one: similarity favours lookalike pages, but the goal is reaching a new audience. Both are exactly the measurement and network questions I want to study at CSH.”
