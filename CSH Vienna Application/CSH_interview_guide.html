Two things you’ll learn at CSH, in simple words

Network science

Studying things as dots and links: people, pages or ideas (dots) and how they connect (links). It shows who is connected to whom, which groups form, and how things spread, like a rumour moving from one group to another.Your example: the Artemis AI page network, and in Paper 3, how narratives spread between platforms

Causal inference

Proving that A causes B, not just that they happen together. Ice-cream sales and sunburn rise together, but the sun causes both. Methods like difference-in-differences compare a group that was affected with one that wasn’t, before and after.Your example: did a policy event cause a change in mood, or was it already changing?

4. Your dissertation, simplyMSc Data Analytics, University of Portsmouth, 2025 · supervisor Dr Ella Haig · Distinction

In one sentence

You used AI to measure how the UK public felt about economic policy, from comments on X, Reddit, YouTube and Quora.

Why

People react online first, but across many sites. One view could warn government early (your Chevening idea, built small).

What you did

  1. Collected 255,446 comments (APIs and archives).
  2. Labelled them with VADER (a word list).
  3. Trained BERT and a BiLSTM, tested them (McNemar, bootstrap), forecast mood with Prophet.

What it showed

  • Mood dropped at the 2022 mini-budget.
  • YouTube calmer, X angrier.
  • Anger and fear came with negative posts (NRC).

What you realised later

BERT looked 88% accurate, but it was checked against VADER’s own labels, not people. And you assumed every platform was the same. That became your paper.
Say this“My MSc measured UK public mood from 255,446 comments with VADER and BERT. BERT looked 88% accurate, but it was checked against VADER’s own labels, and I assumed platforms were equal. My paper fixes that.”
More detail, only if they ask
  • Scores: BERT 88% (macro-F1 0.878), BiLSTM 82%, logistic regression 71%, VADER 65%. All measured against the VADER labels, which is the problem.
  • Hardest label: “neutral”, often confused with “negative”.
  • Tests: McNemar (do two models make different mistakes?) and bootstrap (how sure are we?); chi-square (does mood differ by topic?).
  • Confidence: temperature scaling, so “90% sure” means right 90% of the time.
  • Forecasting: Prophet, trained to May 2025, tested on June 2025.
  • Emotions and explanations: NRC word list for emotions; LIME and integrated gradients show which words drove a decision.
  • Records: some numbers in the text don’t match the code (279,000 vs 255,446 comments). Your paper lists them openly. Always say 255,446.
  • Ethics: approved by Portsmouth as low risk (TETHIC-2025-111094).

The 6 steps (tap through)

How your simulation works

A simulation is a computer experiment where you already know the true answer. Yours is in Python, public on GitHub.

  • Bias, noise, cut-offs: each site makes people sound harsher or softer, varies, and draws its lines differently.
  • Result: just averaging was wrong on 13.8 of 60 days; adjusting, 0.8.
  • Note: real data must estimate each site’s bias (steps 1–5).

7. Artemis AI, simplyCo-founder and CTO · team of eight · beta on 15 Dec 2026

In one sentence

Artemis AI helps creators and businesses understand their social media pages: what people say, what works, when hostile comments spike, and who to collaborate with.

The data

20 pages, 3M+ comments, plus images and video, from the Meta Graph API, stored in an S3 / Iceberg data lake. Images read with CLIP and OCR, video with Whisper.

The AI

Claude labels and explains, DeepSeek learns the rules, your own models (XLM-R, DeBERTa) learn from both, people check samples. 48 models planned.

The network

A map of how pages relate (affinity graph), grouped with Louvain community detection, to suggest collaborations. Early stage.

The page network, as a picture

Dots = pages. Each colour is a community: pages whose audiences and content look alike, found by Louvain community detection.
Solid lines = strong similarity (affinity) inside a community; dashed grey = weak bridges between communities.
Gold line = a suggested collaboration. The goal is a partner that opens a new audience, not a lookalike, so good matches often cross communities.

How the network is built

Page data from the warehouse→Signals: topics, content types, image fingerprints (CLIP), formats, posting times→Similarity between every pair of pages→Affinity graph→Louvain communities→Match score 0–100, top 5 partners

Illustrative picture, not real pages. Only consenting pilot pages and page-level data are used; Meta does not give commenter identity. Status: an early prototype; communities and the match score are being built. At CSH you would learn the network science to do this properly (diffusion, bias in networks).

What it taught you (the research lessons)

Agreement can lie

Two AI models (Claude and DeepSeek) agreed more while both were getting worse. So you check AI against people, not against other AI.

Labels drift

One measure fell from 77% to 37% in weeks without anyone noticing. So you monitor labels all the time.

“Normal” is hard

Out of 207,192 comments on busy posts, almost none were real hostile storms. Every page needs its own baseline.

Words depend on the group

Banter, dialect and emoji (like 😂) get mistaken for abuse or flip labels, so you built filters and a language check.
Say this“At Artemis AI we label millions of comments with AI. I learned labels can look reliable exactly when they’re failing. Checking whether AI labels are right is what I want to study at CSH, and I want network science to do our page network properly.”

The system, left to right: Meta Graph API → collectors → S3 / Iceberg data lake (Athena) → weekly AI labels (text, image, video) → nightly scores → live service → the app and Art-E. Tap a group below to see its models.

  • Status: 2 of 35 product models finished, 11 in progress. Say “48 models planned, being built for the beta”.
