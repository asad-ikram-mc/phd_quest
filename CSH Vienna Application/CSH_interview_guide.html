3. Your paper, explained simply“Is the Platform Part of the Measurement?” · Ikram & Haig · arXiv 2610.16757 · preprint, not yet peer reviewed

The problem, as a picture

The idea in one line

Survey researchers know people answer differently by phone and on paper, and test for it before comparing. Your paper says platforms are “modes” too, so test them the same way.

Where it came from

Your dissertation: 255,446 comments on 6,650 posts about six UK economic topics. The paper openly reports your model was checked against labels from the same word list, and that some dissertation numbers don't match the records. Openness is a strength.

The six steps, one at a time

The headline result (simulation, 500 repeats, 60 days each)

Just add platforms
~14 days
Correct each first
< 1 day

Days when the overall mood pointed the wrong way, out of 60. That is nearly a quarter of days, versus almost none.

Honest limits (know these)

  • People choose platforms, so “who posts” mixes with “how the platform shapes posts”.
  • Four platforms, UK, English only.
  • Measures expressed feeling, not support for a policy.
  • Method plus simulation; real-data study next (1,000–1,500 comments labelled by people, a second annotator).

If they ask

  • Your part? I built the simulation and the dissertation case study; wrote it with Dr Haig.
  • Published? A preprint on arXiv; peer review next.
  • What's new? Others treated annotators or prompts as error sources; not the platform itself.
  • Next? Run it on fresh data, then add AI models, time and narrative spread.
Your paper in 20 seconds“People write differently on Reddit than on YouTube, so AI reads their opinions differently. Adding platforms together can flip the answer: in my simulation the overall mood pointed the wrong way on nearly a quarter of days. My paper gives a checklist to test whether a comparison is fair, and a way to fix it.”

4. Your dissertation, explained simply“Cross-Platform Sentiment Analysis of Public Reaction to UK Economic Policies” · MSc Data Analytics, Portsmouth, 2025 · supervisor Dr Ella Haig · Distinction (project 78/100) · ethics approved

Four questions

  • Can AI read public mood on policy?
  • How accurate are BERT and BiLSTM?
  • Can past mood forecast reactions?
  • How does mood vary by topic and time?

What it reported

  • BERT matched labels 88% (BiLSTM 82%); “neutral” hardest.
  • Sharp dip around the Sept 2022 mini-budget.
  • YouTube more positive, X spikier, Quora sparse; X dominated.
  • Anger and fear went with negative posts.

Use these facts

  • Say 255,446 comments (the records).
  • Don't defend exact topic percentages.
  • Labels need checking against people first.
The honest part“The 88% was agreement with labels from the same word list, so it proved less than it seemed. When I rechecked my records for the paper, a few numbers in the text also didn't match. I report all of that openly in the paper, and it is exactly why the paper exists.”

6. What you told CSH in your applicationThe recording is read with these answers. Stay consistent.

Answer 1 · your project, and what went wrong
MSc dissertation on UK economic policy across four platforms. The AI looked very accurate, but it was trained and checked on labels from the same word list (VADER), so the check proved little; and you had assumed “negative” means the same on every site. Lesson: always ask where the yardstick comes from; test that a measure works the same in each group before comparing. It became the paper.
Answer 2 · a CSH paper you liked
Chen, Kmetty, Iñiguez & Omodei (2025), “The public that engages invisibly”, Communication Methods and Measures. People who comment on political YouTube videos are more polarised than people who only watch, so comment-based studies make society look more divided. Your idea: could a small group who donate their data correct millions of public comments? Same shape as your problem: a big, cheap, biased signal and a small, accurate one.
Answer 3 · what you want to study
When can AI-labelled opinions be compared across platforms, AI models and time, and how do we fix them when they can't? Four steps: (1) extend your simulation; (2) collect new posts around fixed events, labelled by several AIs and checked by people; (3) test whether removing the “platform signal” removes real differences; (4) see how the fix changes how narratives seem to spread. Gap you'd fill at CSH: network science of diffusion.
