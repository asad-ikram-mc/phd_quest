CSH’s five questions, with facts that may help

1. What makes the student unique?
Eight years working with social media data in industry (multilingual scrapers across 15 markets at Dubizzle Labs; social listening for global brands and UK government at M+C Saatchi Fluency; co-founder and CTO of Artemis AI), combined with a research question that came from his own MSc. Chevening Scholar; MSc Data Analytics with Distinction (University of Portsmouth, 2025).

2. Persistence when a project was challenging?
In the MSc (255,446 comments from X, Reddit, YouTube and Quora; VADER, BiLSTM and fine-tuned BERT), he found after submission that his validation was circular: BERT had been trained and checked against VADER’s own labels. Rather than leave it, he turned the flaw into a methods paper with Dr Haig, Is the Platform Part of the Measurement? (arXiv, October 2026), written while working full time.

3. An intellectual strength; how he approaches problems?
He asks where the yardstick comes from. The paper borrows tools from survey research (generalizability theory, measurement invariance and DIF, score linking), fixes 15 anchor events in advance before looking at any counts, and includes a Python simulation with known ground truth (naive pooling gets the direction of daily sentiment wrong on 13.8 of 60 days; adjusted, 0.8).

4. Communication and collaboration?
Co-authored the paper with his supervisor; works with analysts, engineers and client teams at Fluency; leads the technical team at Artemis AI; previously led a team of engineers at Dubizzle Labs.

5. Environment where he will thrive?
A structured programme with clear milestones, an interdisciplinary group, and close mentorship in methods he has not yet trained in (network science of diffusion, causal inference). CSH’s government and industry exchanges match his aim of making social media measurement trustworthy for public bodies.

His proposed PhD focus (for context)

When AI-labelled opinion from social media (sentiment, stance, narrative frames) can be compared across platforms, AI models and time, and how correcting it changes conclusions about how narratives spread. Closest CSH fit: Alexander Hoyle (NLP measurement and validation), Elisa Omodei, Fariba Karimi; springboard projects “Understanding narratives at scale” and “Polarization and minority representation on online platforms”.
