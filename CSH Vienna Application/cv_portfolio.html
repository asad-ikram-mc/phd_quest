Profile

I study whether machine labels of online text mean the same thing across platforms, models and time. My MSc dissertation analysed public reaction to UK economic policy on four platforms, and a methods manuscript with Dr Ella Haig brings psychometric tools to machine-labelled sentiment. Since 2018 I have built the data collection and LLM labelling pipelines that analysis of online conversation depends on, and I check machine labels against human ones before anything is built on them.

Research

Ikram, A. and Haig, E. "Is the Platform Part of the Measurement? A Protocol and Simulation Study for Cross-Platform Equivalence of Machine-Labelled Policy Sentiment." Manuscript, complete; not yet published. Code: github.com/asadfix/platform-measurement, Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.23105129. Treats each platform as a survey mode and applies generalizability theory, measurement invariance and differential item functioning, and score linking to machine-labelled text. In a 500-replication simulation with known ground truth, pooling platform scores at face value gives the wrong sign of daily aggregate sentiment on roughly a quarter of days, from measurement differences alone. No peer-reviewed publications to date.
