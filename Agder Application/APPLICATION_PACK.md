## 1. ELIGIBILITY: THIS IS A WAIVER REQUEST, NOT A COMFORTABLE FIT
An early research pass claimed the advert's "120 ECTS" was untraceable and that the faculty's real floor was 90 ECTS, which Asad meets exactly. **Three independent verifiers refuted that, and they were right.** The correction matters:

- The faculty supplementary regulation's "minimum 90 studiepoeng" is **subject-area depth, not degree size**. The Norwegian text requires "utdanning som tilsvarer femårig mastergrad" (education corresponding to a five-year master's degree) and then separately requires at least 90 credits **in the subject area applied for**, with 30 from years 4 and 5.
- Norwegian law defines the degree. Universitets- og høyskoleforskriften (FOR-2024-06-28-1392) ch. 2, in force 1 Aug 2025, normed a master's at 120 ECTS. A one-year UK MSc does not equal a Norwegian master's on its own.
- **HK-dir, not NOKUT, has handled foreign recognition since 1 Jan 2023**, and its baseline is time parity: "one year of standard study time in the country of education is equivalent to one year... (60 ECTS) in Norway." That reads Asad's MSc as **60 ECTS, not the 90 his Portsmouth transcript states.** Do not lean on the 90.

**What genuinely works in his favour, and it is not nothing:**
- UiA's own forskrift § 5.1 is framed on a **five-year total education** and the word "normally" is in the text. Asad has a **four-year BSc plus a one-year MSc = five years**.
- § 5.1 contains an express recognition power: "The faculty may, as a result of special assessment procedures, recognise other educations as equal to the Norwegian MA degree and as basis for admission to the PhD programme."
- The faculty regulation adds two discretionary clauses, including: "In individual cases, admission may still be considered if the PhD committee finds the documentation presented to indicate that the applicant will be able to successfully complete the PhD programme."
- **§ 5.3's list of mandatory refusal grounds does not include a credit shortfall.** The five listed grounds are publication restrictions, restrictive IPR, under a year of work remaining, no supervision capacity, and undocumented funding.
- The **4th/5th-year clause helps him**: because his MSc follows a four-year bachelor, it sits in year five, so all of it counts as 4th/5th-year credit. A 3+1 candidate is worse placed on this clause than he is.
- A **deficiency-remedy mechanism** exists instead of automatic rejection: extra exams or an approved paper, normally within six months of admission.
- Admission need not be final at application time. National regulation makes admission a condition of **taking up** the stipendiat post, and contemplates the admission deadline being set in the employment contract.

**A SECOND REQUIREMENT THE ADVERT UNDERSTATES.** The advert asks only for "an average grade of B or higher". The faculty regulation is stricter: "grade B or better **on the master thesis** and an average mark of B." Two separate tests. The 78 on the masters project is a comfortable pass on the thesis limb. The average across his seven modules (78, 80, 70, 70, 67, 62, 56) is roughly 69, which is Distinction territory in UK terms, but the 56 in Programming for Data Analytics is the one a committee would notice. Be ready for it; do not volunteer it.

**VERDICT: send the email below before writing 5,000 words.** Not because it is hopeless, but because it is an equivalence decision that only the faculty PhD committee can make, and 28 days is too much work to spend on an unanswered question.

## 5. THE OPENING IN THE LITERATURE: THE DENOMINATOR PROBLEM
**Do not re-litigate "does the recommender radicalise".** That question has been asked across four generations of design and the answer is now contingent and well documented:
- **Hosseinmardi, Ghasemian, Rivera-Lanas, Horta Ribeiro, West & Watts (2024)**, "Causally estimating the effect of YouTube's recommender system on solitary, extremist video consumption", *PNAS* 121(8):e2313377121 - post-2019 the recommender is, if anything, a moderating force.
- **Chen, Nyhan, Reifler, Robertson & Wilson (2023)**, "Subscriptions and external links help drive resentful users to alternative and extremist YouTube channels", *Science Advances* - exposure concentrates among the already-resentful and arrives via subscriptions and off-platform links.
- **Horta Ribeiro, Veselovsky & West (2023)**, "The Amplification Paradox in Recommender Systems", *ICWSM* - explains mechanically why sock-puppet audits contradict user-trace studies.
- **Haroon, Wojcieszak, Chhabra, Liu, Mohapatra & Shafiq (2023)**, "Auditing YouTube's recommendation system for ideologically congenial, extreme, and problematic recommendations", *PNAS* 120(50):e2213020120 - problematic recommendations are rare in absolute share (about 2.5%) yet reach over a third of sock puppets.

**Where it stops, and this is the project:** every prevalence and amplification figure in the field is a ratio whose numerator is produced by a content or channel classifier, and those classifiers do not agree with each other or generalise.
- **Yin & Zubiaga (2021)** - cross-dataset generalisation in abusive-language detection has been "severely overestimated".
- **Riabi et al. (COLING 2025)** - annotator disagreement on radical content is systematic and socio-demographically patterned, not noise.
- **Brouillette-Alarie, Hassan et al. (Dec 2025)**, Campbell Systematic Reviews 21(4):e70080 - reviewed 20 studies of six violent-extremism risk assessment tools and found **no true predictive validity established** (all designs retrospective, hence postdictive); ERG22+ field inter-rater kappas 0.47-0.80 against 0.76-0.93 in research settings; "the current state of empirical validation precludes the recommendation of one tool over another".

**And here is the alignment that makes this application worth writing.** NIVE's government mandate, following the Extremism Commission (NOU 2024:3, 41 recommendations) and Meld. St. 13 (2024-2025, 45 measures), includes **developing a common risk assessment tool** for PST, police and frontline services. A national centre is about to build a risk assessment instrument, and the best available systematic review says no existing tool in that family has demonstrated predictive validity. That is a measurement-validity problem sitting on a live national deliverable, in a department that has published the IS agenda for online extremism. Asad's whole record is about instruments that report confidence without criterion.
