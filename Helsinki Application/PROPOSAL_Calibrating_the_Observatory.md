## 1. Background and Motivation

Computational social science has changed its measurement instrument. Within three years, large language models moved from novelty to default annotator for social media text: they beat crowd workers on standard tasks at a twentieth of the cost (Gilardi et al. 2023), and the field followed. What followed too is an uncomfortable evidence base about the instrument itself. Reasonable configuration choices flip the statistical conclusions of published annotation studies about 31 per cent of the time (Baumann et al. 2025). Prompt wording alone moves label distributions by double digits (Camuffo et al. 2026). Proprietary models change behaviour under silent backend updates (Chen, Zaharia and Zou 2024), which is why methodologists warn against building research on APIs whose versions cannot be pinned (Ollion et al. 2024).

Between April and August 2026 the field converged on the diagnosis. Annotation is now argued to be a measurement act, not an agreement exercise (Harvey, Koenecke and Kizilcec 2026); annotation regimes, the pairing of model and prompt, are shown to produce different measurements of the same construct, with regime differences propagating into substantive results (Milkova et al. 2026); persona conditioning is shown to shift what models label as harmful along ideological lines (Civelli et al. 2025); and industry now has decision frameworks for whether a replacement model is safe to adopt (Casey et al. 2026). The diagnosis exists. The calibration discipline does not, and this proposal builds it.

The need is concentrated where monitoring is longest and widest: the **observatory**. The era of single-platform research ended for structural reasons; the 2023 closure of affordable Twitter/X access reversed two decades of growth (Murtfeldt et al. 2024), and whole platforms now carry distinct, persistent ideological profiles, so apparent opinion shifts often reflect users sorting between platforms rather than changing their minds (Di Martino et al. 2026). Any credible opinion signal must therefore pool several platforms, read by LLM instruments, over years. An observatory running 2025 to 2030 will (a) read several platforms with one instrument without evidence it measures the same construct on each; (b) cross many silent model updates, so trend lines confound changes in the world with changes in the instrument; and (c) embed an epistemic standpoint in that instrument, since language models are not neutral readers (Nelimarkka 2026). Survey methodology solved the structural analogues decades ago: mode equivalence testing, bridging designs when instruments change mid-series (Kraemer et al. 2025), and documentation of the instrument's perspective.

I can also show the problem from inside. In my own production annotation systems (3.39 million comments through a multi-model cascade at ArtemisAI, the company I co-founded), measured inter-model agreement is 82.9 per cent for sentiment and 0.0 per cent for toxicity, while the toxicity model self-reports 96.7 per cent average confidence; the dashboard's operational "accuracy" figure is, on inspection of the code, inter-model agreement with no criterion behind it. The zero is not a broken system; it is a label-taxonomy and threshold mismatch between tiers, which is to say an equivalence failure, and that is precisely the thesis. This is the observatory problem in miniature, and I run it daily.

The foundation paper with Dr Ella Haig (Ikram and Haig 2026) supplies the starting machinery: a six-step psychometric protocol treating each platform as a survey mode, and a 500-replication known-truth simulation in which a naive pooled index reports the wrong sign of aggregate sentiment on roughly a quarter of days while a measurement-adjusted index does not. The doctoral project carries that machinery from fitted classifiers to the instruments the field now actually uses.

## 2. Research Aims and Novel Contribution

**Aim.** To develop and empirically validate a calibration discipline for LLM-annotated public opinion measurement across platforms, over time, and across epistemic standpoints, so that longitudinal multi-platform monitoring can distinguish real opinion change from instrument change.

**Four contributions, each stated against its nearest neighbour:**

1. **Platform equivalence of LLM labels, formally tested.** Hou, Thapa and Tay (2026) built the equivalence framework with language as the axis and left platform open. The one platform-portable measure to date (Ameen and Healey 2026) validates by correlation with held-out external scores, which cannot detect scalar non-invariance, the failure mode that flips pooled trend signs in the foundation paper's simulation. The residual this project takes: fitted categorical invariance and DIF models with platform as the grouping variable and a human-coded criterion in the model.
2. **A bridging design for instrument change.** Existing work detects that an API model changed (Gao, Liang and Guestrin 2025), re-validates against fixed audit sets (Cheng, Mayya and Sedoc 2024), and decides when a replacement is safe to adopt (Casey et al. 2026). Nothing corrects the measurement series when versions are not equivalent. This project supplies the correction: a pinned open-weight shadow annotator as a fixed bridge anchor, stratified misclassification-matrix corrections, and a costed re-annotation policy, framed honestly as the cost-bounded alternative to re-annotating an entire archive at every update.
3. **Standpoint as a measurement facet.** The supervisor's MarxistLLM (Nelimarkka 2026) builds standpoint-conditioned instruments and evaluates them qualitatively; persona work shows ideology shifts labels (Civelli et al. 2025) without decomposing the effect. Here, standpoint configurations enter a multivariate generalizability analysis whose reported objects are the covariance structure among standpoints and fixed-effect contrasts per construct, with platform crossings testing whether standpoint effects travel. The aim is not to average standpoints away but to make the instrument's perspective visible and reportable (Klein and D'Ignazio 2024).
4. **The criterion test: does calibration pay?** The state of the art in platform-based opinion estimation corrects selection and stops there, on a single platform (Cerina and Duch 2026). This project runs the missing horse race with the selection layer held identical across all index arms, so the comparison isolates the measurement contribution: naive pooled versus platform-specific versus calibrated indices, nowcasting probability-sample survey series. Either result is publishable.

No paper or group holds more than one of these cells; the observatory is what makes them one project. Statistical-correction methods fix estimates within one corpus and one instrument version (Gligorić et al. 2025; Egami et al. 2023); reliability scoring stops at reliability (Barrie, Palaiologou and Törnberg 2024); variance-aware protocols decompose pipeline facets without platform, time or standpoint (Camuffo et al. 2026).

## 3. Research Questions

1. **RQ1 (Equivalence).** Do LLM-scored opinion constructs achieve threshold and loading invariance across platforms discussing the same policy events, and where invariance fails, which items function differentially and by how much?
2. **RQ2 (Drift).** How large are the measurement discontinuities introduced by model updates in a running annotation pipeline, and can a shadow-anchored bridging design keep a multi-year opinion series comparable across versions at a stated fraction of full re-annotation cost?
3. **RQ3 (Standpoint).** How large and how stable are the effects of epistemic standpoint conditioning on LLM-scored opinion measurements, and do those effects travel across platforms?
4. **RQ4 (Criterion).** With selection correction held identical, does a measurement-calibrated multi-platform index nowcast survey-measured opinion better than a naive pooled index, and by how much?

## 4. Literature Context

**The instrument turn and its instability.** LLM annotation was legitimised by accuracy claims (Gilardi et al. 2023) and destabilised by their examination: conclusion-flipping configuration sensitivity (Baumann et al. 2025), regime-dependent measurements (Milkova et al. 2026), and the recognition that annotation is measurement (Harvey, Koenecke and Kizilcec 2026). The field's remedies are one-shot criterion validation and statistical correction against gold labels; all of it treats the instrument as fixed and the corpus as one population.

**Time, drift and the observatory.** Model behaviour shifts under silent updates (Chen, Zaharia and Zou 2024); detection (Gao, Liang and Guestrin 2025), audit sets (Cheng, Mayya and Sedoc 2024) and migration decisions (Casey et al. 2026) exist. Media observatories are being built with serious infrastructure and no instrument-versioning methodology (Pehlivan et al. 2025). Survey research bridges instrument changes mid-series with parallel administration (Kraemer et al. 2025); that design has never been ported to AI annotation, where the archive's re-annotatability changes the economics but not the logic.

**Standpoint and whose measurement.** Language models encode political and cultural positions (Santurkar et al. 2023), and the supervisor's programme has pushed this from complaint to instrument: MarxistLLM asks whether a model can analyse from a declared theoretical perspective (Nelimarkka 2026), and the group's earlier work showed different computational eyes see different things in the same images (Berg and Nelimarkka 2023). Missing is the measurement bridge: standpoint as a designed facet whose effects are estimated, not assumed.

**The supervisor's programme is the natural home.** The group's credo, the role of theory and the importance of validity and reliability in computational analysis, is this project's method; Platformed Interactions (Nelimarkka et al. 2020) is the in-group demonstration that platforms shape political communication; the Finnish Digital Observatory (2025-2030) is the running system whose honesty this project underwrites.

## 5. Methodology

**5.1 Design.** One **data spine**, four studies, four papers. The construct family is public attitudes toward government economic policy, chosen for three reasons a reviewer should hear: it is the best survey-anchored construct family at monthly-or-better frequency in both countries; its anchor events (budgets, fiscal statements) are calendar-scheduled, giving pre-registrable windows no culture-war topic offers; and it extends the applicant's published measurement base rather than starting cold. The design is deliberately asymmetric: the **UK is the development arm** (the foundation paper's fifteen frozen fiscal events; weekly YouGov government-economic trackers as the rolling criterion, British Election Study waves for validation), and **Finland is the confirmatory arm** (anchor set co-designed with the supervisor around budget riihi and election moments; candidate criterion series named now: Statistics Finland's monthly consumer confidence indicators, EVA's attitude surveys, and Eurobarometer trust-in-government items). Platforms: Reddit, YouTube and Bluesky, selected for currently credible research access with a pre-registered density audit and a named substitution rule. Two corpus layers: a low-volume **continuous keel** (a fixed keyword and actor panel, a few thousand items per day, running the entire project, which is what Study 2's live pipeline annotates) and high-density **anchor windows** on top. Everything is archived under a provenance discipline: every raw item stored, every label logged with model identifier, endpoint, prompt hash, temperature, seed and timestamp, and a frozen probe set re-scored on schedule as the drift tripwire.

**5.2 Data and access.** Collection uses the applicant's production tooling, which is the feasibility argument in one sentence: he currently operates, for enterprise clients including Amazon and Ford, a pipeline ingesting ten platforms daily into a governed 156-column master table and a 68-crawler fleet across 62 domains that collected 6.7 million items in six weeks under CI quality gates, KL-divergence drift alarms and operator-approved deletion controls. YouTube access is pursued via the researcher programme and via DSA Article 40 vetted-researcher status, for which a University of Helsinki affiliation qualifies and whose systemic-risk framing matches the project's civic-discourse constructs; Reddit via Reddit for Researchers; Bluesky via the AT Protocol firehose with archived captures. The human criterion: a stratified sample of 1,000 to 1,500 items per country, double-coded with 25 to 30 per cent overlap under a pre-registered multi-item codebook; Finnish annotation by native-speaker coders recruited through the group, with the codebook translated and back-translated, which is one of the concrete reasons this project belongs in Helsinki.

**5.3 Studies.**
- **Study 1, Equivalence (UK arm from month 5, Finnish arm from month 9; through month 18).** The measurement model, stated: the unit is the post; the construct is scored by the LLM on a pre-registered multi-item codebook (policy stance, intensity, blame attribution), so the items are codebook sub-constructs and the LLM is the scorer, not the item; the human criterion sample enters the factor model as a second method, so shared machine misreading is detectable rather than invisible. Estimation: ordered-categorical CFA under WLSMV with theta parameterisation; the invariance sequence follows Wu and Estabrook (2016), thresholds before loadings, with dMACS-class effect sizes rather than fit-index worship; DIF with iterative anchor purification, alignment as the identification check. The platform-nesting confound is broken by a common-item linking bank: cross-posted texts, matched pairs, and platform-context prompt manipulations on identical texts, converting the confounded comparison into a common-item design. Two model families and a pre-registered prompt battery throughout; repeated draws at fixed settings estimate the stochastic floor first, so DIF is not fitted to sampling noise.
- **Study 2, Drift (months 12-30).** Two regimes, both powered by design rather than vendor courtesy. The backbone is a **pinned open-weight version ladder**: at least six planned version transitions the applicant executes as designed interventions with full parallel-run overlap on the keel. Vendor-forced and silent proprietary updates are opportunistic replications, handled by the second mechanism: a **continuously running pinned open-weight shadow annotator** on a rolling stratified reference sample, which serves as a fixed bridge anchor that no vendor can retire, plus retrospective re-annotation of stratified archive samples by the post-update model once the frozen-probe tripwire fires. The correction estimator is a stratified misclassification-matrix adjustment within pre-registered content strata (hundreds of items per stratum per event), with the stationarity assumption stated and tested; the whole design is framed as the cost-bounded alternative to re-annotating the archive at every update, with the cost curve computed from real numbers (re-annotating a 6.7-million-item archive per update versus roughly one to two million calls per bridging event). This extends monitoring the applicant already ships in production, where weekly KL-divergence alarms watch pipeline output distributions.
- **Study 3, Standpoint (months 18-34, jointly with the supervisor).** Zero new collection: the frozen spine is re-annotated under standpoint conditions (baseline instruction, declared-perspective variants in the MarxistLLM tradition, and a formal-neutral control), crossed with model family and prompt on a fractional design, full crossing on one flagship platform-country pair. Analysis is a **multivariate generalizability study** in the Brennan (2001) tradition: standpoint is a fixed facet, so the reported objects are the covariance structure among standpoint-conditioned scores and fixed-effect contrasts per construct, a mixed-model decomposition rather than a random-effects generalisation claim; the declared residual is run-to-run variance at fixed settings. Platform crossings test whether standpoint effects travel, which is the invariance question at the second level.
- **Study 4, Criterion (months 26-42).** Naive pooled, platform-specific, and calibrated indices, with the **selection-adjustment layer held identical across all arms** in the Cerina and Duch tradition, so only the measurement layer varies; rolling-window nowcasts against the weekly UK tracker as the powered comparison and the Finnish series as replication; human panels treated as one more method under MTMM logic (Cernat et al. 2025), with the survey criterion as the external terminus. A null result, calibration does not pay, is itself publishable in the post-Gayo-Avello literature.

**5.4 Validation, robustness and staging.** Effect-size criteria and sensitivity analyses across model families and prompts throughout; open-weight models pinned for reproducibility, proprietary models handled through the Study 2 machinery rather than trust. Declared drop-order if time compresses: Study 3 folds into Study 1 as an additional facet; Study 4's design is locked at month 26 because the adjacent literature is moving. Months 43-48 are reserved for thesis compilation, revisions and defence, which is the honest core of the four-year feasibility argument.

## 10. Conclusion

The field rebuilt its measurement stack on instruments that change silently, read differently on every platform, and carry a standpoint nobody quantifies, and it did so exactly when multi-platform, multi-year monitoring became the only viable design. Between April and August 2026 the literature converged on this diagnosis; what it still lacks is the discipline that survey methodology built for exactly this situation two generations ago. This project ports that discipline, with its identification details written down rather than gestured at. The applicant brings a published psychometric protocol, six years of production platform-data engineering, and daily operational contact with the problem, from a ten-platform production pipeline to a multi-model cascade whose agreement pathologies this project would finally explain. The Helsinki Social Computing Group is the one place where the measurement machinery, the standpoint theory and the running observatory already sit in the same room.

### Summary (cap 1,000) - 996 characters

Computational social science now measures public opinion by having large language models read social media, pooled across platforms over years. Three properties of this instrument are untested: whether its scores mean the same thing on every platform, whether they stay comparable when the model is updated, and how much they depend on the standpoint built into the model. This project develops and validates the missing calibration discipline. Four studies share one data spine (UK and Finnish economic-policy discourse, three platforms, survey anchor series): fitted measurement-invariance and DIF tests with platform as the group; a shadow-anchored bridging design that keeps a series comparable across model updates; a multivariate generalizability analysis of epistemic standpoint; and a criterion test of whether a calibrated multi-platform index nowcasts surveys better than naive pooling. Deliverables: an open toolkit and reporting standard, piloted with the Finnish Digital Observatory.

### Objectives and expected impact (cap 1,500) - 1362 characters

Aim: to develop and empirically validate a calibration discipline for LLM-annotated public opinion measurement across platforms, over time, and across epistemic standpoints, so longitudinal multi-platform monitoring can distinguish real opinion change from instrument change.

Objectives. O2 (Drift): quantify measurement discontinuities from model updates and validate a shadow-anchored bridging design that corrects the series, at a stated fraction of full re-annotation cost. O3 (Standpoint): estimate, in a multivariate generalizability design, how large and how stable standpoint-conditioning effects are, and whether they travel across platforms. O4 (Criterion): with selection correction held identical, test whether a calibrated multi-platform index nowcasts survey-measured opinion better than naive pooling.

Impact: the first calibration methodology for AI-instrumented opinion measurement; an open toolkit and a reporting standard adoptable by observatories, regulators exercising DSA data access, the survey profession, and industry; and operating methodology for the Finnish Digital Observatory across its 2025-2030 model updates.

### Methods and materials (cap 4,000) - 3435 characters

One data spine, four studies, four papers. Construct family: public attitudes toward government economic policy, chosen because it is the best survey-anchored construct family at monthly-or-better frequency in both countries, its anchor events are calendar-scheduled (pre-registrable windows), and it extends the applicant's published measurement base. Asymmetric arms: the UK is the development arm (fifteen frozen fiscal events published in the foundation paper; weekly YouGov trackers as rolling criterion; British Election Study waves for validation); Finland is the confirmatory arm (anchors co-designed with the supervisor; candidate criterion series: Statistics Finland consumer confidence, EVA surveys, Eurobarometer trust items). Platforms: Reddit, YouTube, Bluesky, under a pre-registered density audit with a named substitution rule; YouTube access additionally via DSA Article 40 vetted-researcher status. Two corpus layers: a continuous keel (fixed keyword and actor panel, a few thousand items per day, running the whole project) and high-density anchor windows. Human criterion: 1,000 to 1,500 items per country, double-coded with 25 to 30 per cent overlap; Finnish annotation by native-speaker coders recruited through the group.

Study 1, Equivalence (months 5-18). Unit: the post. The construct is scored on a pre-registered multi-item codebook (policy stance, intensity, blame attribution); the LLM is the scorer, not the item, and the human criterion enters the factor model as a second method so shared machine misreading is detectable. Ordered-categorical CFA under WLSMV, invariance sequence per Wu and Estabrook (2016), dMACS effect sizes, DIF with iterative anchor purification. The platform-nesting confound is broken by a common-item linking bank: cross-posted texts, matched pairs, and platform-context prompt manipulations on identical texts. Two model families, a pre-registered prompt battery, stochastic floor estimated first.

Study 2, Drift (months 12-30). Backbone: a pinned open-weight version ladder, at least six planned transitions executed as designed interventions with full parallel overlap on the keel. Silent and vendor-forced updates are handled by a continuously running pinned shadow annotator on a rolling stratified reference sample plus retrospective re-annotation once the frozen-probe tripwire fires. Correction: stratified misclassification-matrix adjustment within pre-registered content strata, stationarity stated and tested; framed as the cost-bounded alternative to full archive re-annotation.

Study 3, Standpoint (months 18-34, with the supervisor). Zero new collection: the frozen spine re-annotated under standpoint conditions (baseline, declared-perspective variants in the MarxistLLM tradition, formal-neutral control) on a fractional design. Analysis: multivariate generalizability study (Brennan 2001); reported objects are the covariance structure among standpoint-conditioned scores and fixed-effect contrasts; platform crossings test whether effects travel.

Study 4, Criterion (months 26-42). Naive pooled, platform-specific and calibrated indices with the selection-adjustment layer held identical across arms; rolling nowcasts against the weekly UK tracker, Finland as replication; human panels as one method under MTMM logic. A null result is publishable.

Drop-order if time compresses: Study 3 folds into Study 1 as a facet; Study 4's design locks at month 26.

### Data plan (cap 1,000) - 906 characters

Public platform content only, collected via official and researcher APIs into university and CSC storage. Every raw item is archived; every label carries a provenance record (model identifier, endpoint, prompt hash, temperature, seed, timestamp), and a frozen probe set is re-scored on schedule as the drift tripwire; this archive is what makes retrospective bridging possible. Retention follows a documented schedule with platform deletion-propagation compliance, a control the applicant already operates in production. Identifiers are hashed at ingestion; quotations are paraphrased in outputs. Derived aggregates, codebooks, model configurations and analysis code are published openly with each paper; raw text is shared only under the platforms' terms and GDPR safeguards. Data management follows University of Helsinki and Finnish Social Science Data Archive practice, with a DMP finalised in month 3.

### Ethics (cap 1,000) - 872 characters

Political-opinion texts are special-category data under GDPR Article 9. The legal basis chain is Article 9(2)(j) scientific-research processing under Article 89 safeguards as implemented by the Finnish Data Protection Act (1050/2018) research derogations, with a data protection impact assessment in Year 1, building on the applicant's ethics-approved MSc procedures (University of Portsmouth, TETHIC-2025-111094) re-approved under University of Helsinki review before collection. No private or restricted content; hashed identifiers; aggregate reporting; paraphrased quotations so posts are not re-identifiable by search; annotators paid and briefed, with wellbeing safeguards for exposure to hostile content. Platform terms of service govern collection modes per platform, and the density audit absorbs the resulting asymmetries by design rather than circumventing them.
