## The ten lessons

1. **Map every stated criterion to evidence, explicitly.** Before drafting, copy the advert's profile and the "we look for" sentences into a checklist. Each item needs a sentence of evidence in the letter, written in the advert's own words. VU lost on four criteria he could have met.
2. **Lead with research and analysis, never with collection infrastructure.** Both rejected letters opened on scrapers. A social-science committee reads "500+ collectors" as "data engineer". This was fixed for IWM and UvA (analysis first, collectors one line); keep it that way. Section 3 of the email pattern is amended to match.
3. **Show interest in their phenomenon before your method.** "The question transfers directly to your project" reads as a pre-existing agenda looking for a host. The SDU adversarial review found the same thing. Open on their problem and their papers, then bring the method.
4. **Use the assets you actually have:** the economic-policy dissertation, the Dubizzle revenue-prediction dashboards, Urdu (native) plus multilingual text work in Arabic and Hindi, codebooks and close reading of posts as qualitative work, and four commercial clients' analysis.
5. **Stop promising arXiv dates.** "On arXiv shortly" has been in letters since August and the preprint is not posted, which a committee can check. Say "complete, not yet submitted" until it is live. Getting it posted (Ella's sign-off) is the single biggest fix to the profile: "no publications" appears in every gaps paragraph.
6. **Screen eligibility and fee status before writing a word.** UK "home fees only" studentships, social-science-degree-only posts and supervisor-first systems have each cost real time.
7. **Big open calls are lotteries; weight effort accordingly.** ELLIS had 1,400+ applicants and VU 550+. Prefer posts with a warm or named supervisor, smaller pools, and topics where his data-plus-measurement profile is rare. Contact the named person where the advert invites questions.
8. **Time cold outreach to the cycle.** September to November for the next year's entry. March and April is too late.
9. **Follow up on day 14, every time.** Put the nudge on the calendar the day an email goes out, using the pattern's two-sentence follow-up with one new fact. Three good leads went cold because this did not happen.
10. **Ask for feedback once, briefly, after a rejection.** Utrecht invites questions at recruitment@uu.nl. A two-line request costs nothing and is the only way to learn what the generic rejections hide.
