## THE RULES

1. **No em-dashes.** Hyphens only.
2. **Always open with:** "I am writing to enquire about PhD opportunities at [institution] for [year] entry."
3. **Hook on the professor's OWN named theory or paper**, never a generic description of their field.
4. **Attach** `Asad_Ikram_Full_Resume_2026.pdf` before sending.
5. **Verify the email address** on the university's official contact page before sending.
6. **Once the arXiv paper is live, always link it.** Until then: "on arXiv shortly" with the project page asadfix.github.io/publish_proposal/#paper.
7. Never send without passing all six checks below.

---

## THE SIX CHECKS (before any draft is finalised)

1. Does the email name the professor's **own theory, framework or specific paper**?
2. Does the dissertation finding **land inside that theory**?
3. Is ArtemisAI's commercial gap expressed as **the professor's unsolved research problem**?
4. Does the bridging question use **the professor's exact vocabulary**?
5. **The Nelimarkka test:** would the claim survive "the fact that platforms differ is not novel"? The email must start where their literature stops (equivalence of the measurement, not existence of the differences) and show you have read what THEY cite, not just what they wrote.
6. **The AI test:** read it aloud. No "Three parts." fragments, no em-dashes, no symmetrical triads, contractions where natural. If it sounds like a model wrote it, redraft.

If any answer is no, the draft is not ready.

## THE EIGHT-PART STRUCTURE

### 1. OPENING + HOOK

```
Dear Professor [Surname],

I am writing to enquire about PhD opportunities at [institution / lab name]
for [2026 or 2027] entry. I wanted to ask whether my background connects to
the research direction you pursue on [professor's named concept], and
specifically your argument in [named paper or book] that [their core claim
in their own words].
```

### 2. CHEVENING + THE GOVERNMENT PROBLEM

```
My Chevening Scholarship was built around a specific professional problem:
social media analytics pipelines are already being used to inform government
decision-making, but the underlying measurement systems cannot [the specific
failure that THIS professor's work identifies].
```

### 3. FLUENCY + DUBIZZLE + FIX.COM PROOF

```
I have spent six years building those pipelines. At M+C Saatchi Fluency I
built the data infrastructure that processes Sprinklr social media data daily
for UK Government departments alongside Amazon, Ford, Nike and Reckitt. At
Dubizzle Labs I led a team running over 500 regional scrapers across MENA. At
Fix.com I processed 50 million data points from more than 200 sources.
[One closing line tying this back to the professor's framing.]
```

### 4a. THE DISSERTATION

```
My MSc dissertation (University of Portsmouth, Distinction) built a
cross-platform NLP framework analysing 279,000 posts across Twitter, Reddit,
YouTube and Quora on UK economic policy using fine-tuned BERT (macro-F1 0.878),
BiLSTM, NRC emotion analysis, LIME explainability and Temporal Fusion
Transformer forecasting. The central finding was a platform divergence problem:
the same policy event produced structurally different sentiment and engagement
profiles across platforms.
```

**The three dissertation numbers are always the same: 279,000 posts, BERT macro-F1 0.878, four platforms.**

### 4b. THE PAPER (the strongest card - use it in every email from now on)

```
That finding is now a methods paper with my MSc supervisor Dr Ella Haig:
"Is the Platform Part of the Measurement?" treats each platform as a survey
mode and specifies a six-step psychometric equivalence protocol
(generalizability theory, measurement invariance and DIF, score linking) for
machine-labelled sentiment. Its simulation result: across 500 replications, a
pooled cross-platform index that takes platform scores at face value reports
the wrong sign of aggregate sentiment on roughly a quarter of days, purely
from measurement differences; a measurement-adjusted index does not.
[On arXiv shortly / arXiv:XXXX.XXXXX once live.] Then one line landing the
protocol inside the professor's framework.
```

**The paper numbers are always the same: 500 replications, wrong sign on about a quarter of days, misstates a known event shift by about an eighth, 23 pages.**
**Never mix the two sets: 279k/0.878 belong to the dissertation, 500/quarter/eighth to the paper.**

### 5. ARTEMISAI TIED TO THE PROFESSOR'S GAP

```
I am also CTO and co-founder of ArtemisAI (https://www.artemisai.co.uk), a
UK-based social media intelligence platform currently in early access
development. ArtemisAI runs its NLP models simultaneously across brand social
data through a six-model NLP suite with a three-tier LLM fallback cascade,
3.39M comments ingested, CLIP multimodal fusion and video intelligence, built
on AWS SageMaker and Redshift. When useful, the concrete detail: in production
our two model tiers agree on 82.9% of sentiment labels and 0.0% of toxicity
labels, while the toxicity model reports 96.7% average confidence. [The problem their research identifies theoretically is the
problem ArtemisAI faces operationally every day: we cannot say what our
pooled scores measure.]
```

### 6. THE BRIDGING QUESTION

```
The question I want to pursue at doctoral level is whether [restate the
research question using the professor's exact terminology], and if so, what
[the methodological contribution] would look like in practice.
```

This paragraph separates a strong email from a generic one. Use their words, not yours.

## THE PITCH-PAGE ESCALATION (new in v2)

For a warm-but-skeptical contact, a supervisor a live call depends on, or anyone
who has already pushed back once, an email is not enough. Build a one-page
answer site and link it from the email:

- Quote their exact critique and answer it under their own words.
- Map the state of the art in a visual (clusters, what each established, where
  each stops, the gap in a highlighted box).
- Show the paper as stat cards, not prose.
- Timeline the plan (work packages on a Gantt), and put the ask against the
  call's real dates.
- Give them ownership of one work package by name.

Worked example (Nelimarkka, Helsinki call): asadfix.github.io/helsinki_proposal.
Cost: about a day. Use sparingly; it signals seriousness precisely because it
is not what everyone does.

## THE FOLLOW-UP (2 sentences + one new fact)

A follow-up must contain **one concrete fact that changed** since the first
email (the paper posted, an acceptance, a new result). Never a bare nudge.

```
Dear Professor [Surname],

I wanted to follow up on my email from [month] regarding PhD opportunities.
Since then, [the one new fact: e.g. the paper I mentioned is now on arXiv:
(link)]. I remain very interested in your work on [their concept] and would
welcome any thoughts on fit.

Best wishes,
Asad Ikram
```

## THE WARM-REPLY RESPONSE (3-4 sentences)

Used when a professor replies positively but has no current opening:

```
Dear [First name],

Thank you very much for taking the time to reply, and for the kind words about
the framing.

I will keep a close eye on [their website / the call] for [the specific window
they mentioned]. By that point the methods paper from my dissertation, written
with my MSc supervisor, will be on arXiv [link once live], which I hope will
strengthen the application considerably.

I look forward to getting in touch again when the call opens.

Best wishes,
Asad Ikram

[full signature]
```

**Why this works:** it is short, it does not push, and it gives them something concrete that will have changed by the time you contact them again.

## THE CRITIQUE-REPLY (new in v2 - when a professor pushes back)

Learned from the Nelimarkka exchange (his 7 April "what concretely" and 1 May
"not novel - follow the state of the art"):

1. **Do the homework before replying.** Months of silence with the work done
   beats a fast reply with promises.
2. **Quote their words back and answer under them.** Their critique is the
   outline of your reply.
3. **Read what they cite, not only what they wrote.** Name the works their
   paper actually points to.
4. **Move the claim one step past their literature**, and say exactly where
   theirs stops and yours starts.
5. If the stakes justify it, escalate to a pitch page (above).

## THE LATE-REPLY OPENING (learned from the Ella email)

When replying embarrassingly late: apologise once, give the real reason in
one or two plain sentences (ArtemisAI + two jobs), then immediately show the
work you did instead of replying. The work is the apology. Never a paragraph
of excuses, never AI-polished contrition.

## COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID

1. **Generic hook.** "Your work on social media" is not a hook. "Your 2017 argument that Twitter metrics measure attention rather than support" is a hook.
2. **Describing their field back to them.** They know their field. Show you have read a specific paper.
3. **Leading with the CV.** The dissertation, the paper and the infrastructure work are evidence for an argument, not the argument itself.
4. **Claiming novelty without the state of the art.** "Platforms differ" got called not novel by a lecturer in the field, correctly. The claim is equivalence of the measurement, and it must be positioned against what they cite.
5. **Asking them to solve your problem.** The email frames it as: here is a problem you have identified, and here is why I am positioned to work on it.
6. **Em-dashes.** Hyphens only.
7. **AI-sounding prose.** Fragments for drama, perfect triads, "delve", "landscape". Read it aloud first.
8. **Forgetting the CV attachment.** Every email says "I have attached my CV." Make sure it is actually attached.
9. **Unverified email address.** Two addresses were wrong and bounced. Check the university contact page.
10. **A follow-up with nothing new in it.** One new fact, or wait until there is one.
