## 1. WHAT THE TWO-PAGE PROPOSAL SHOULD PROPOSE

### The angle, in one sentence

**Same score, different source: SP1 cannot say how the amount and character of negativity "differ across news outlets, social media platforms, and individual news diets" until it has established that one negativity instrument measures the same thing in all three places, and put those scores on a common scale.**

That is the whole proposal. It is not a new question bolted onto SP1, it is the precondition of SP1's own stated question, which is exactly the room to "affect the direction" that a fixed-design advert leaves open. It also happens to be the only thing on this project that Asad is better placed to do than anyone the Centre could otherwise hire, and it converts his one real intellectual asset (the Haig manuscript) into a service to the project rather than a critique of it.

Frame it as a service throughout. The register is: *your design is right, and here is the measurement work it needs in order to be believed.*

### Why this angle and not the alternatives

- The negativity measure is the input to everything. SP2 asks how people navigate negative news, SP3 asks what exposure to it does to beliefs and well-being. Both consume an SP1 number. If that number is not comparable across the sources composing a person's diet, SP3's exposure variable is not comparable across people whose diets are differently composed, and SP3 finds a null or a wrong sign. Scharkow & Bachl (2016) already showed content-analysis error drives minimal-effects findings in exactly this linkage design, in Political Communication. That gives the argument a home in their literature, not in ML.
- The team has no computational member (Andersen, Heiselberg, Hopmann, Brems: survey/psychometric, psychophysiological/qualitative, comparative content analysis, manual content analysis). The advert was re-scoped from DQR014 to DSL655 with "strong computational profile" added. The scarce commodity in the room is computation. The scarce commodity in Asad's application is theoretical fluency. The proposal must therefore spend its ink on constructs and sampling, not on architecture.
- The hubs are the funded headline. The Velux record calls "data donation hubs" *en central metodisk nyskabelse*. The advert calls managing them "a crucial task". Infrastructure competence is the position, not a chore attached to it. But infrastructure alone is not a PhD, so the hub must be presented as **the acquisition instrument in a measurement design**, which is what makes it publishable (Computational Communication Research) rather than administrative.

### The shape: one governing question, four named sub-studies

SDU and Aarhus proposal guidance both want a programme: one theme statable in a sentence, several subordinate projects, feasibility visible. Use this skeleton.

**Governing question.** How much negativity, and what kind, do Danish news outlets publish, do platforms surface, and do individual feeds actually contain, and can those three quantities be measured on a scale that makes the comparison meaningful?

**WP1. A Danish negativity instrument that is multidimensional and validated per source.**
Codebook from Lengauer, Esser & Berganza's three axes and the five DOCA coding variables (negative tone toward actors, overall tonality, pessimistic outlook, conflict-centredness, incapability/misconduct), coded at actor-in-story or claim level following Hopmann, de Vreese & Albæk (2011), not at document level. Separate valence from discrete emotion (Soroka, Young & Balmas 2015: negativity, fear and anger are distinct). Gold standard annotated by Danish-native coders, two coders minimum, iterated coding list in the Heiselberg & Hopmann (2024) idiom. Fine-tune a multilingual encoder; benchmark honestly against AFINN, SENTIDA, DaNLP BERT Tone and an LLM first pass. Report Krippendorff's alpha per construct per source alongside macro-F1. Deliverable: the first validated Danish news negativity measure, which to my knowledge does not exist.

**WP2. The hub as instrument.** Single-visit hub protocol, DDP parsing with an automated repair layer, selective redaction, resolution of donated IDs back to text, and full funnel instrumentation so the hub itself yields a methods paper. Detail in section 4.

**WP3. The invariance and linking study.** This is the flag-planting sub-study. Test whether the WP1 instrument is measurement-equivalent across outlet article text, platform post text, and donated feed items; model document length as an explicit facet, because Chan et al. (2021) found the dominant latent factor in 37 dictionary scores across 2.2m NYT articles correlates r = -0.933 with article length, and length is precisely what differs between an article, a Facebook post and a TikTok caption; test differential item functioning by outlet, platform and format; use score linking so a feed-level negativity score sits on the same scale as an outlet-level one; propagate classifier error into the aggregate estimates SP2 and SP3 consume (Egami et al. 2023 show 80-90% surrogate accuracy still produces substantial bias and invalid intervals, and ship the `dsl` R package).

**WP4. The substantive payoff.** Do individual Danish feeds actually contain more negativity than the outlets that fed them? This is a real open question and it is *Kim Andersen's own public claim*: his Politiken op-ed of 30 March 2025 says platforms amplify negative and dramatic stories, while his own Paradox working paper finds the negativity advantage in engagement decays within seconds. Neither his surveys nor the ESMP simulated feed can settle it. Donated feeds can. Then hand SP3 a person-day exposure variable with a stated error model, shaped the way Hopmann, Vliegenthart, de Vreese & Albæk (2010) shaped daily content data for joining to rolling cross-section survey data: state the temporal granularity, the join key and the aggregation unit explicitly.

### What must NOT be attempted

Hard prohibitions. Each of these either poaches a colleague's territory, claims capability Asad does not have, or trips a known scepticism.

1. **Do not propose building a feed simulator, recommender testbed or experimental social media platform.** That is the ESMP / SDU Social, it is Rasmus Schmøkel's delivered PhD, and it is next door at the Digital Democracy Centre. Name the DDC as an intended collaboration instead. Low cost, high signal.
2. **Do not propose qualitative interviewing, focus groups or psychophysiological work.** SP2 is Heiselberg and Brems. Offer the emotion layer as a *bridge* to them (does a machine label "negative" correspond to what a reader reports feeling), not as his study.
3. **Do not propose fine-tuning a generative LLM, building an agent, or an LLM-as-primary-labeller design.** He has never fine-tuned a generative model. LLM output is a first-pass labeller that must itself be validated against a Danish human gold standard, and SDU's own DAISY benchmark (Nielsen, Beltoft, Schneider-Kamp & Galke Poech, arXiv 2601.19930, all four at SDU) shows frontier models perform poorly on Denmark-specific content. Cite that; it is an internal citation and a possible internal collaborator.
4. **Do not propose a single scalar sentiment score.** "Amount and character" is a direct lift from Lengauer et al. in the same Journalism 13(2) special issue where Hopmann co-wrote the sibling review on balance. A scalar collapses all three of Lengauer's axes at once and will read as illiterate to this panel.
5. **Do not propose open-web scraping as a method.** Content resolution is resolving items the donor has already consented to donate, under SDU governance. Say it in those words.
6. **Do not claim representativeness for donated feeds**, and do not propose to fix it by weighting alone. Propose benchmarking: outlet corpus as the supply frame, donated feeds as personalised exposure, SP3's representative panel as the anchor for bounding the bias (this is Boeschoten et al.'s own recommended mitigation for consent error, and it uses infrastructure the project already has).
7. **Do not lead with scale.** No "68 collectors", no "279,000 posts", no "500+ collectors" in the proposal. Andersen and Larsen publicly demolished the Danish "uge 46" convention on sampling-frame grounds and publicly corrected a widely repeated 92% statistic. Volume without a stated population and sampling frame is the thing most likely to irritate him. State the frame first, then the size.
8. **Do not treat negativity bias as settled.** His newest choice experiment finds a *preference for positive news*. Treat amount and character of negativity as an open empirical and measurement question. Mention preregistration.
9. **Do not add an SP4 or redefine the project.** Stay anchored in SP1, as the advert instructs, and say once that methods from SP2 and SP3 will be drawn on for validation and benchmarking.
10. **Do not claim human-subjects, IRB or DPIA experience.** Ethics work is named, planned, and learned under supervision. Say that plainly.

## 2. THE HONEST NOVELTY POSITION

After the adversarial line, the claim is **partially occupied** and must be narrowed. What is occupied: that automated sentiment measures lose validity out of domain (canon since Ribeiro 2016, González-Bailón & Paltoglou 2015, made definitive by van Atteveldt, van der Velden & Boukes 2021 in Communication Methods and Measures, where every dictionary sits near chance and Lexicoder reaches ordinal alpha 0.33); that instruments disagree with each other on news (Boukes et al. 2020, tool-to-tool alpha below .35); that instrument choice can flip a substantive conclusion (Reiss & Roggenkamp 2025 on Robertson et al. 2023); and that imperfect machine labels bias downstream inference within one setting (Scharkow & Bachl 2016; Knox, Lucas & Cho 2022; Egami et al. 2023). What is not occupied, on structured searching of OpenAlex and arXiv: measurement invariance, DIF or score linking applied to the output of an automated content-analysis instrument. The psychometric vocabulary and the computational-content-analysis vocabulary have not met.

**The two sentences a committee would accept, to be used close to verbatim:**

> That automated negativity measures lose accuracy when moved between domains, genres and languages is the field's own established premise (van Atteveldt et al. 2021; Ribeiro et al. 2016; Chan et al. 2021), and that imperfect machine labels bias downstream estimates has been demonstrated within a single estimation setting (Scharkow & Bachl 2016; Egami et al. 2023). To my knowledge, no study has yet treated this as a measurement-equivalence problem: whether one negativity instrument is equivalent across news outlet text, platform posts and individual donated feeds, and how those scores can be linked onto a common scale, which is the precondition that SP1's core comparison silently assumes.

Three rules attached to that statement.

- **"To my knowledge", never "no study has".** The gap rests on targeted searching, not a systematic review, and conference papers (ICA, COMPTEXT) are poorly indexed.
- **The Haig manuscript is a motivating pilot, not evidence.** Describe it as an unpublished, unsubmitted working paper, on English-language UK economic policy text, not replicated on Danish data. The 500-replication result (a pooled cross-platform index reporting the wrong sign of aggregate sentiment on roughly 25% of days) can appear once, in that frame, as the reason the question is worth asking. It must never appear as an established finding, and never in a first sentence.
- **Name Chan's length confound before a reviewer does.** A reviewer will ask why cross-platform negativity differences are not simply an article-length artefact. Say it first, and make length a modelled facet in WP3. Doing so converts the single most dangerous fact in the literature into evidence that he has read it.

Two useful positioning moves that cost nothing. First, Boukes et al.'s Table 6 shows correlation with human coding rising sharply with temporal aggregation (LIWC headlines r = .26 per article, .41 per day, .60 per week) but *falling* for weak instruments (DANEW full text .18 to -.15). That is the same aggregation/measurement-error structure as the Haig simulation, so cite Boukes as the precedent and position the simulation as the formal treatment of when aggregation rescues and when it deceives. Second, Watson et al. (2024, Scientific Reports) is the ideal worked example for the opening: a good paper in a good journal running VADER over both full articles and tweets and concluding articles are "less negative than the tweets that concern them", with no test that the two scores are on the same scale, using an instrument built for social media text. Treat it as an illustration, never as an attack.

## 4. THE HUB ENGINEERING: WHAT ASAD WOULD ACTUALLY DO

This is the section that must be specific, because it is the part a journalism centre genuinely cannot hire and the part where seven years of production collection stops being a CV line and becomes a method. Six nameable deliverables, each with its failure mode stated. In the proposal these compress to a WP2 paragraph plus a sentence or two elsewhere; in the motivated application they get more room.

**(a) A schema-drift detection and repair layer for DDPs.**
Golden-fixture corpus of known-good download packages per platform per locale; contract tests over parser output; versioned parsers with the parser version stamped onto every extracted record; weekly canary donations from project-owned test accounts on each platform in both Danish and English locale; alerting when a fixture stops parsing. *Failure modes this defends against, all documented:* DDP folder and JSON key names change with the account's language setting, which is lethal in Denmark where donors' phones split between Danish and English; Facebook exports come as .json or .html; Instagram's 2020 media-plus-JSON layout became 2022 text-files-in-folders with a JSON-or-HTML choice; WhatsApp exports differ structurally between Android and iOS including timestamp notation; Garmin relocated heart-rate fields mid-campaign between 9 and 21 June 2023 and forced donors to resubmit; a policy change produced silently empty files; an unrecognised date-time notation causes partial extraction that looks like success. The field's entire published response to this is manual firefighting, described by Gómez Ortega (2025) as "invisible labor". Nobody has proposed an automated defence. Asad has been paid to build exactly this for seven years. This is unclaimed methodological ground.

**(b) A content-resolution layer, which is the thing that decides whether SP1 exists.**
Donated YouTube entries are video IDs, TikTok gives links not titles, Instagram gives IDs and omits URLs and titles. A negativity classifier cannot touch any of it. Every donated item must be resolved back to text, caption or transcript at population scale before analysis. Hase et al. state that researchers must supplement DDPs with APIs and that "most platforms shut down or drastically restrict their APIs", which renders donation studies unfeasible for content analysis. That is the field naming a blocker. *Design:* resolution queue with rate limiting, exponential backoff, per-item provenance logging, content-hash caching so an item resolved for one donor is not re-fetched for the next, and a documented lawful basis (resolving content the donor consented to donate, under SDU governance, not open-web scraping). *Failure modes:* deleted, private or geo-restricted items; anti-bot interception; silent partial resolution that biases the resulting exposure measure toward content that is still popular and still live, which is a survivorship bias directly correlated with the outcome. That last one must be named and measured (report resolution rate by platform, by item age, by outlet), not just mitigated.

**(c) A single-visit hub protocol, empirically grounded rather than assumed.**
The published two-visit problem (request the package on visit one, donate on visit two) exists because platform delivery used to take days. Karnam et al. (IEEE S&P 2026) measured TikTok delivering instantaneously at 6 clicks, Instagram 6 clicks in 10-15 minutes, YouTube 10 clicks in 10-15 minutes. If that holds in Denmark in 2026, a single-visit hub becomes feasible: approach, request, coffee, extract on device, inspect, consent, donate, leave. *This must be proposed as a claim to verify per platform in Denmark, not asserted.* de León et al. (2026) show experimentally that attrition is driven by delivery latency rather than privacy fear, which is the argument for the hub. *Failure modes:* download links expire (Facebook and TikTok roughly four days, WhatsApp 30, Garmin three), so a donor who leaves before the package arrives is usually lost for good; a full-history request takes far longer to deliver than a one-year window, and the requested window is not admin, it redefines the construct (a week of Instagram watch history and a year of Facebook measure different things).

**(d) Selective redaction that preserves outlet identity.**
Standard de-identification pipelines replace everything after an @ with a code. Van Driel et al. report exactly this, and note it also erased company handles and locations, and that covering all faces made emotion-from-image analysis impossible. For SP1 this is fatal: if the handle following the @ is the news outlet, blanket redaction erases the variable the entire "which outlets are more negative" question depends on. *Design:* public-entity allowlist covering Danish news brands, verified media accounts and public institutions (Brems's outlet map is the natural starting frame), salted-hash pseudonymisation of private-person handles, redaction validated against a re-identification risk assessment as part of the DPIA. *Failure modes:* allowlist staleness as outlets rebrand or new alternative outlets appear; third parties in the donor's data (tags, DMs, uploaded contacts) who cannot consent; donors who used the account when under the platform's minimum age.

**(e) On-device extraction with a two-stage consent surface.**
Build on Port (Boeschoten et al. 2023, JOSS), which runs the researcher's Python extraction script inside the participant's browser via Pyodide so the raw package never leaves the device, or on OSD2F (Araujo et al. 2022, archived and superseded), or on the current D3I stack at datadonation.eu. Say by name which one and why. The extraction script is Python, browser-side, which is the toolchain Asad already writes in. Consent is genuinely two-stage: enrol, then consent again after inspecting the extracted preview. *Failure mode:* donors curate. Wedel & Ohme (2026) find omission concentrated among first-time donors and show it significantly compromises completeness. Boeschoten et al. warn this consent error is likely missing-not-at-random and "could be related to topics of interest measured within the DDPs", meaning donors who saw the most negative feed may be exactly the ones who withhold it. *Turn it into a study:* does a face-to-face hub increase omission (felt observation) or decrease it (trust in the researcher, which Keusch et al. find predicts donation success at +2 percentage points per scale point)? That is a clean, cheap embedded experiment and nobody has run it.

**(f) Instrumenting the hub so it produces data about itself.**
Log every funnel step with timestamps: approach, consent to enrol, request submitted, platform delivery latency, extraction attempted, per-file parse outcome, omission events, final donation, per-item resolution outcome. Without this there is no yield analysis, no comparison against the published funnels (Keusch: 79% willing, 48% success; van Driel: roughly one in four who start), and no methods paper. With it, the hub is publishable in Computational Communication Research as the first evaluation of assisted physical data donation. To my knowledge no published study has evaluated a public data-donation hub; the literature is unanimous that assistance should help and has no evidence on how much, for whom, or at what cost.

**Operational realities worth one sentence each in the application:** it must work on a stranger's phone, iOS and Android, with no app install; a multi-gigabyte export on shopping-centre wifi with a phone that is low on storage and battery; an upload queue tolerant of dropped connections; no project staff ever touching a donor's credentials or logging into a donor's account; a Danish-language consent sheet and Danish-speaking hub staff. And the boring one that matters most to a supervisor: a versioned, reproducible pipeline (parser version + model version + corpus hash on every record) so a number printed in a 2028 paper can be regenerated in 2030.

## 5. HOW TO HANDLE THE DANISH PROBLEM IN WRITING

**Principle: name it unprompted, once, early-ish but never first, and framed as a design parameter with a research question attached rather than as a deficit with an apology attached.** Silence reads as not having thought about it. Defensiveness reads worse. The panel does not require Danish for this post (the SQM's PhD row requires only fluent English or Danish for teaching; the Danish requirements attach to teaching-track promotion at 2-5 years, and SDU provides Danish tuition), so the risk is not eligibility, it is whether he looks like someone who understands what language does to a measurement instrument.

**In the research proposal: one sentence in the theory framing and two or three in methods.** Framing sentence: language is a third axis of non-transfer alongside domain and genre, so an instrument built for English social media and applied to Danish news copy is a triple domain shift, and the design treats language as one more facet in the invariance test rather than as a nuisance handled once at the start. Methods: the annotation and validation architecture, stated as a division of labour.

**The concrete claims to make, all of which are verified:**
- On Danish data, lexical tools collapse on exactly the formal-to-informal transition SP1 depends on (AFINN 0.68 Europarl to 0.48 Twitter; SENTIDA 0.67 to 0.44), while transformer-based tone models hold (BERT Tone 0.79 to 0.73). This is an argument, with Danish numbers already on the table, for a multilingual-encoder route plus in-language validation.
- No published Danish sentiment tool is validated on Danish news. SENTIDA's domain validation used 45 texts, 15 each from fiction, politics and social media, averaging 37 words, annotated by six people including the authors, and its own limitations section concedes this. DaNLP's evaluation sets are 184, 499 and 512 sentences. That gap is the contribution, phrased as "to my knowledge no validated Danish news negativity measure has been published".
- Danish pretraining data is thin on news (Gigaword news is 3.92%, from two sources) and its social-media portion is dominated by a single horse forum, so domain-adaptive pretraining on the project's own corpora is justified rather than gold-plating.
- DaCy large is built on XLM-RoBERTa, the exact family Asad has fine-tuned in production. Say this once, plainly. It is the single most transferable technical fact he has.
- DaCy's augmentation-based robustness protocol (keystroke errors at 2/5/15%, æøå-to-ae/oe/aa substitution, lowercasing) is a ready-made validation regime for a small Danish gold standard, and donated feed text is exactly where casing and spelling noise bite.
- LLM-assisted annotation is a promising first pass for Danish that must be validated against a human Danish gold standard before use. Rathje et al. (2024, PNAS) is encouraging but did not test Danish; SDU's own DAISY benchmark points the other way on Danish-specific content. That is a research question, not a caveat.

**The division of labour, stated explicitly rather than hidden:** Danish-native colleagues and trained student coders (the Centre has roughly 310 BA and 200 MA journalism students and a taught methods programme) own codebook construction and gold-standard annotation in Danish. Asad owns the codebook-to-classifier pipeline, the hub infrastructure, the fine-tuning, and the measurement-validity layer: intercoder reliability targets, annotation learning curves answering how much Danish annotation is actually needed, per-outlet and per-platform DIF, score linking, and error propagation. Add one safeguard sentence: a bilingual adjudication protocol and an explicit check that a Danish-trained classification head has not silently imported English-language decision boundaries, with negation and irony probes (van Atteveldt's error analysis found a Naive Bayes model learning "bankruptcy" and "unemployment" as *positive* features because they usually appear negated in news phrasing, and a CNN classifying "more bankruptcies" as positive).

**In the motivated application: one short paragraph, positioned after the capability paragraphs, not before.** Say he does not read Danish; say what he will therefore not do (own the codebook, own the Danish gold standard, adjudicate ambiguous Danish items); say what the design does about it; and commit concretely to Danish tuition from arrival with a stated target (functional reading of Danish news copy within twelve months, using SDU's provided resources). One sentence, one commitment, no hedging, then move on. Do not say "I am willing to learn Danish" without a target and a mechanism.

## 7. THE THREE BIGGEST RISKS, AND THE HONEST MITIGATION

**Risk 1: The proposal reads as an engineering plan with theory bolted on, and fails Research on fit rather than on capability.**
This is the likeliest single failure mode. The matrix's PhD research criterion is "ability to define a promising PhD project **within the department's research portfolio**". The dominant epistemology in that room is survey, manual content analysis, experiment and interview, with heavy Danish policy engagement. Nobody there will out-engineer Asad; they will out-argue him on construct validity. Andersen publishes in Communication Research, Journal of Communication and IJPP, and his contribution to this literature is complicating claims others assert.
*Mitigation:* the ordering rules in section 6, enforced ruthlessly. Cite six of the panel's own papers. Report kappa first. Name the sampling frame before the corpus size. Cite van Dalen's dictionary work as precedent rather than deficiency. Cite the Cores-Sarría working paper as a baseline to extend rather than a target to hit. Delete every sentence that would only impress an ML reader. If a paragraph could appear unchanged in an NLP workshop paper, it is wrong for this document.

**Risk 2: The formal eligibility gate, specifically the 120 ECTS line, decided by a chair doing document-matching before any scholar reads the science.**
A UK one-year taught MSc is 180 UK credits, conventionally mapped to 90 ECTS, not 120. The advert says "120 ECTS **or equivalent documented qualifications**", the governing executive order (BEK 1124 of 19/09/2025, §5) requires admission to be based on a master's programme "in terms of level", and the PhD School, not the department, makes the admission call after an offer. But the shortlisting chair sees only the documents, five days from now.
*Mitigation, in this order:* (a) state it proactively and factually in the application, giving the UK credit values for both the bachelor and the MSc, the total study load, the Distinction, and the dissertation mark, so the chair is never left to guess and never has to reject on ambiguity; (b) quote the advert's own "or equivalent documented qualifications" phrasing back, and cite the executive order's level test rather than a credit count; (c) attach the Portsmouth transcript and, if the university can produce anything in five days, a statement of study load; (d) email Kim Andersen before the deadline, briefly and professionally, asking both about degree equivalence and whether the earlier DQR014 posting was a separate SP2/SP3 post or an earlier round of this one. The answer to the second question changes how competitive the field is. An official assessment from the Danish Agency for Higher Education and Science cannot be obtained in five days; do the documented version now, and start the official one immediately after submitting so it exists before any offer.

**Risk 3: Novelty overreach on the measurement claim, in front of a panel constitutionally hostile to overclaiming.**
The premise (instruments do not transfer) is canon in the exact journal these people publish in. Chan et al. got to 90% of Asad's argument within one outlet across instruments. If the proposal presents domain specificity as a discovery, or leads with the 25% wrong-sign figure as a finding, it will read as under-read to the two people most likely to have read all of it. There is also a live sub-risk: if Hopmann or anyone on the team has already published on comparability of automated negativity measures, the framing must change from "gap" to "extension". That was not resolvable in the research and is the highest-value thing left to check.
*Mitigation:* use the two-sentence position verbatim; write "to my knowledge"; label the manuscript unpublished and unsubmitted every time it appears; state that the simulation is on English UK economic policy text and has not been reproduced on Danish data; name Chan's length confound yourself and make length a modelled facet; and describe every near-neighbour paper (Watson, Robertson, Cores-Sarría, van Dalen) as good work whose comparison rests on an untested assumption, never as a mistake. Attach the manuscript with its co-authorship declaration so the claim is documented rather than asserted, which is also what turns it into assessable evidence under the SQM.

**Also live, below the top three:** zero publications against a field that may include applicants with two or three (mitigated by the SQM's explicit acceptance of documented unpublished work, and by the fact that the post was re-scoped toward computation because they did not find the profile they wanted); the proposal possibly being read anonymised, which the stand-alone rule already handles; and five days of writing time, which argues for drafting the proposal first, at full quality, and building the motivated application from its offcuts.
