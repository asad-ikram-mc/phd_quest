Master's thesis

Cross-Platform Sentiment Analysis of Public Reaction to UK Economic Policies. Collected ~279,000 public posts on UK economic policy from X/Twitter, Reddit, YouTube and Quora via official APIs and licensed feeds, under ethics approval (Portsmouth TETHIC-2025-111094) and GDPR-compliant procedures. Fine-tuned a BERT classifier to macro-F1 0.878 against BiLSTM and lexicon baselines; human validation at Cohen's κ ≈ 0.78; temperature-scaling calibration; McNemar and bootstrap significance testing; NRC emotion analysis linking anger and fear to negative policy reactions; LIME for interpretability; Prophet forecasting of the daily sentiment index (MAE ≈ 0.02). The finding that shaped my research interest: the same policy event produced structurally different sentiment and emotion profiles on different platforms, which meant the cross-platform aggregate rested on an untested assumption, that the classifier measures the same construct everywhere it reads.

Research output

Ikram, A. and Haig, E. “Is the Platform Part of the Measurement? A Protocol and Simulation Study for Cross-Platform Equivalence of Machine-Labelled Policy Sentiment.” Manuscript, complete; not published and not submitted. Treats each social platform as a survey mode and applies generalizability theory, measurement invariance and differential item functioning, and score linking to machine-labelled text. In a 500-replication simulation with known ground truth, an index that pools platform-level scores at face value reports the wrong sign of the aggregate on roughly a quarter of days, purely from measurement differences between platforms.
