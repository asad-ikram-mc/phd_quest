## 2. THE FIELD AS IT STANDS, FAILURE MODE BY FAILURE MODE

The call names six failure modes in objective 1. They are at very different stages of maturity, and the proposal must not treat them as equally open.

**Evidence laundering / synthetic provenance — MEASURED ONCE, EARLY, TRACTABLE.** Allaham & Diakopoulos, "Synthetic Sources?: Auditing Generative Search Engine Citations for Evidence of AI-Generated Sources," arXiv:2605.23684, 22 May 2026: ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Perplexity over 712 real queries in politics, health and environment; **~16% of cited sources show evidence of being AI-generated**; engines concentrate on a narrow repeatedly-cited domain set with a long tail of minimally-cited ones. *Settled:* the phenomenon exists and is measurable. *Open:* longitudinal drift, cross-lingual replication, and a validated measurement model for that 16% rather than a single-shot point estimate with an unaudited detector. Note the detector problem — "AI-generated source" is itself a machine judgement with unknown sensitivity and specificity. Nobody in Pierri's group has done this.

**Source omission and citation faithfulness — ACTIVE, WITH AN ESTABLISHED AUDIT INSTRUMENT.** DeepTRACE, arXiv:2509.04499, audits GPT-4.5/5, You.com, Perplexity, Bing Copilot and five deep-research agents on eight dimensions: one-sided answer, overconfident answer, unsupported statements, uncited sources, citation accuracy, citation thoroughness, source necessity, relevant statements — with the judge validated against humans on 100 items (Pearson ≈ 0.72 on the reported dimension). *Settled:* generative search engines routinely make statements their cited sources do not support, and the dimension set is now conventional. *Open:* whether these rates are stable across languages, over time, and across harnesses; and whether the judge's own error rate materially biases the reported rate.

**Overconfident synthesis — SETTLED AT THE HUMAN LEVEL, IN ONE MODALITY.** This is what the Overreliance paper found: confidence flat at 4.5/5 while accuracy moves ±32 points. That is a calibration failure located in the *user*, not the model. *Settled:* users do not discount for a deceiving assistant. *Open:* everything about longitudinal exposure, personalisation, and whether the effect survives when the user has interaction history with the agent.

**Bias amplification / biased source distributions — WELL MEASURED IN NON-AGENT SETTINGS, THINLY IN AGENTS.** Minici et al. (arXiv:2510.27489) find reduced outlet diversity and model-specific skews in cited sources, consistent with distinct implicit editorial behaviours. Dai et al. (EMNLP 2025) find the media source matters more than the content in producing political bias in LLM citations. Park & Kim (EMNLP 2025) find and mitigate outlet-name bias. In Pierri's own idiom, the gold standard is Bär et al.'s ad-delivery work: not a benchmark score, but a demographic delivery discrepancy on billions of impressions across 25 jurisdictions with confounders controlled. *Open:* the same rigour applied to what an agent retrieves and cites, at population scale.

**Sycophancy — THE CONSTRUCT ITSELF IS BROKEN, AND THAT IS DOCUMENTED.** "What Counts as AI Sycophancy? A Taxonomy and Expert Survey of a Fragmented Construct," arXiv:2605.21778: different studies use the same label for behaviours differing in form, mechanism and measurement, and critically **SycEval and ELEPHANT produce inverted rankings for the same models**. Syco-bench reports inter-test correlations r < 0.3 across its four subtests. *Settled, and this is important for you:* the cross-instrument invariance failure on sycophancy has already been demonstrated, without any latent-variable model. *Open:* a construct definition that survives, and any longitudinal or memory-mediated account of sycophancy. Pierri has nothing on sycophancy; his nearest object is the prisoner's-dilemma paper (Fontana, Pierri, Aiello, ICWSM 2025), where Llama2 and GPT-3.5 are more cooperative, forgiving and non-retaliatory than typical humans below 30% opponent defection — excessive accommodation framed game-theoretically rather than epistemically.

**Susceptibility to adversarial or manipulated web content — DEMONSTRATED IN A SINGLE CONTROLLED SHOT.** The deceiving-assistant arm of the Overreliance experiment is the demonstration, in one modality, with one proprietary model, on Anglophone crowdworkers, in round 3 of 3. *Open:* nearly all of it — content manipulated in the environment rather than in the assistant, multi-turn, cross-lingual, and with any realistic adversary model.

**Temporal drift as a distinct thing from run-to-run noise — ALREADY SEPARATED ONCE.** "Characterizing Web Search in the Age of Generative AI," arXiv:2510.11560, separates temporal drift (model and web-content change) from stochasticity (run-to-run variation) in generative search, with experiments repeated roughly two months apart.

## 3. WHAT ALREADY EXISTS — BENCHMARKS AND AUDIT METHODS A COMMITTEE EXPECTS YOU TO KNOW

**Agent benchmark validity.** Zhu et al., "Establishing Best Practices for Building Rigorous Agentic Benchmarks," arXiv:2507.02825 — the 43-item Agentic Benchmark Checklist; 8 of 10 popular agentic benchmarks show severe validity issues; **do-nothing agents pass 38% of τ-bench airline tasks**; augmented tests change **41% of SWE-bench rankings**; misestimation up to 100% in relative terms. Caban, "Measurement Without Validity: The Compounding Reliability Problem in Agentic AI Evaluation," arXiv:2608.00794 — construct validity, inter-rater reliability and content validity fail simultaneously, with a multiplicative compounding model across pipeline layers. And the meta-level is taken too: "Auditing the Audit: Five Failure Modes in Benchmark-Validity Audits," arXiv:2607.02586. *Limitation:* these are checklists and diagnoses, not measurement models, and none of them controls the information environment.

**The harness literature — know this or lose credibility instantly.** Harness-Bench, "Measuring Harness Effects across Models in Realistic Agent Workflows," arXiv:2605.27922; "Stop Comparing LLM Agents Without Disclosing the Harness," arXiv:2605.23950; "'LLM Agent Performance' Is Not a Single Evaluation Target," arXiv:2602.03238. Magnitudes: the Holistic Agent Leaderboard shows single-model swings up to ~48 points on SWE-bench Verified Mini across scaffolds; at fixed model, changing harness shifts pass@1 by **8.5–13.0 points**, while at fixed harness changing the model shifts it **2.5–5.0 points**; Claude Opus 4.5 on SWE-bench Pro scores 45.9% under SEAL and 55.4% under Claude Code. **For agents, the harness moves the score more than the model does.**

**Psychometrics applied to LLM evaluation — a live, crowded sub-field.** Ye, Jin, Xie, Zhang, Song, "Large Language Model Psychometrics: A Systematic Review of Evaluation, Validation, and Enhancement," arXiv:2505.08245 (v3, 11 Mar 2026), **explicitly lists measurement invariance and DIF as core properties of the programme**. Also: "Auditing LLM Benchmarks with Item Response Theory," arXiv:2605.30504 (114 models, seven benchmarks); "Lost in Benchmarks? Rethinking LLM Benchmarking with Item Response Theory," arXiv:2505.15055 (PSN-IRT, 12 LLMs, 11 datasets); "Measuring what Matters: Construct Validity in LLM Benchmarks," arXiv:2511.04703; "Rating the Raters: Rasch Measurement Theory for LLM Evaluation," arXiv:2608.27463 (many-facet Rasch over nine LLM annotators).

**Imperfect-label statistics — solved, packaged, shipping.** Chaganty, Mussmann, Liang, "The price of debiasing automatic metrics in natural language evaluation," ACL 2018, arXiv:1807.02202 (control variates; and the sobering result that this saved only 7–13% of annotation cost). Angelopoulos, Bates, Fannjiang, Jordan, Zrnic, "Prediction-Powered Inference," *Science* 2023, arXiv:2301.09633, plus PPI++. Boyeau, Angelopoulos, Yosef, Malik, Jordan, "AutoEval Done Right," arXiv:2403.07008. Egami, Hinck, Stewart, Wei, "Using Imperfect Surrogates for Downstream Inference: Design-Based Supervised Learning," NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2306.04746 — **LLM surrogate labels at 80–90% accuracy still produce substantial bias and invalid CIs**, with a doubly-robust fix and a public R package (`dsl`). Wei et al., "Can Unconfident LLM Annotations Be Used for Confident Conclusions?," arXiv:2408.15204, NAACL 2025. Lee, Zeng et al., "How to Correctly Report LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluations," arXiv:2511.21140 — bias-corrected accuracy from the judge's sensitivity and specificity, with CIs carrying uncertainty from both the test set and the calibration set. Plus arXiv:2605.06939, 2601.20913, 2606.05308, 2608.26638. **Most damaging of all for a 2026 proposal: GLIDE, "Industrializing Prediction-Powered Inference... for Reliable GenAI and Agentic Systems Evaluation," arXiv:2605.31278** — stratified PPI for *agentic* system evaluation, already an engineering product.

And outside CS this is roughly forty years old: the imperfect-gold-standard literature in diagnostic epidemiology — Hui-Walter models, Bayesian latent class models for sensitivity/specificity with no gold standard, conditional-dependence extensions (BMC Med Res Methodol 2023, doi 10.1186/s12874-023-01873-0), Schofield et al. *Statistics in Medicine* 2021, MetaBayesDTA (arXiv:2211.05949), QUADAS-2.

**Judges of research agents specifically.** Wang, He, Chen, Yehudai, Liu, Ying, Shmueli-Scheuer, Cohan (Yale + IBM Research), "Time to REFLECT: Can We Trust LLM Judges for Evidence-based Research Agents?," arXiv:2605.19196, May 2026. 472 verified reference-corrupted execution pairs (140 process/reasoning, 132 tool-use, 200 report-level) across eight failure categories, expert-validated at **κ = 0.86**, judges tested under pointwise, pairwise and best-of-N interfaces. **Best judges are below 55% accuracy overall and are worst on evidence verification specifically.**

**Institutional data sources that do not require Article 40 vetting.** Trujillo, Fagni, Cresci, "The DSA Transparency Database: Auditing Self-reported Moderation Actions by Social Media," PACM HCI (CSCW) 9(2), Article CSCW187, Apr 2025, DOI 10.1145/3711085 — 353.12 million records from the eight largest EU platforms in the database's first 100 days. Cresci is a frequent Pierri co-author. Also relevant as a feasible collection environment: Bluesky and the fediverse, where Pierri's group already works (Bono, Liu, Russo, Pierri, ICWSM 2026, arXiv:2505.01174; Bono, La Cava, Luceri, Pierri, WebSci 2024; Nogara et al., *Online Social Networks and Media*, Nov 2025).

## 4. THE HONEST NOVELTY POSITION

**You cannot claim as new:**
- That benchmark scores are not comparable across model versions. Cacioli, "Beyond the Mean: Within-Model Reliable Change Detection for LLM Evaluation," arXiv:2604.27405, 30 Apr 2026, did it with the Reliable Change Index on 2,000 MMLU-Pro items, K=10 at T=0.7 (80,000 trials), split-half reliability, SE of difference. Llama 3→3.1 +1.6 pts, Qwen 2.5→3 +2.8 pts, and the aggregate gain is "the net residual of opposing item-level movements": 34% of items improved and 28% deteriorated (Llama), 47%/39% (Qwen); greedy single-shot evaluation missed 42% of reliably changed items.
- That DIF changes model rankings. Zheng & Yang, "Are Near-Tied LLM Rankings Robust to Family-DIF-Guided Benchmark Recomposition?," arXiv:2609.00482, **submitted 31 August 2026 — eighteen days before this deadline**. τ-b .900–.948 between full and low-DIF rankings, yet **30.9–47.1% of near-tied model pairs reverse order in four of five benchmarks.**
- That generalizability theory fixes LLM-pipeline measurement error. Messing, "Hidden Measurement Error in LLM Pipelines Distorts Annotation, Evaluation, and Benchmarking," arXiv:2604.11581 — G-theory variance decomposition plus decision studies on Chatbot Arena and MMLU, items as object of measurement, prompt variants / judge model / temperature / replications as facets; naive SEs are 40–60% too small; halves MMLU estimation error. **That is the "Is the Platform Part of the Measurement?" manoeuvre already executed on LLM evaluation by someone else.** Reinforced by "Variance-Aware LLM Annotation for Strategy Research," arXiv:2601.02370 (explicitly G-theory, five variance sources, design choices shifting outcomes 12–85 percentage points).
- That LLM-judge error propagates into reported rates. See the whole PPI/DSL/bias-correction stack in section 3, and GLIDE for the agentic case.
- That the sycophancy construct is not invariant across instruments. arXiv:2605.21778 documented inverted rankings between SycEval and ELEPHANT.
- That psychometrics is a useful lens on LLMs. arXiv:2505.08245 is a systematic review that names measurement invariance and DIF as sub-areas of an existing programme.

**People already occupying the measurement-theory × LLM-evaluation intersection, by name:** Solomon Messing (G-theory / D-studies on LLM pipelines); Jon-Paul Cacioli (reliable change / classical test theory on version comparison); Qiaoyuan Zheng and Yiqu Yang (IRT-based DIF on benchmark recomposition); the authors of "Rating the Raters" (many-facet Rasch); Ziyu Ye, Jin, Xie, Zhang and Song (the LLM-psychometrics review); Naoki Egami, Musashi Hinck, Brandon Stewart and Wendy Wei (design-based supervised learning); Anastasios Angelopoulos, Stephen Bates, Michael Jordan, Tijana Zrnić (prediction-powered inference); Chaganty, Mussmann and Liang (the 2018 ancestor); and, in the diagnostic-epidemiology lineage that predates all of them, the Hui-Walter / Bayesian LCM community.

**What you can honestly claim.** Not the method. The **object**. Every one of the papers above varies the model, the judge, the prompt, the persona, the temperature or the harness, because those are free to vary. **Nobody varies the retrieved evidence set under experimental control**, because that requires building and holding a corpus of web content with known provenance, known source-quality distribution, known contamination, and the ability to serve it to a live-browsing agent as if it were the open web. That is web-collection engineering, and it is exactly the capability a psychometrician does not have. It also sits inside objective 3 (controlled experiments with open models and modular agent architectures) rather than dangling off objective 2.

You can also honestly claim a **fit** argument that is unusual and true: Pierri's group has named the annotator-validity problem in its own limitations sections three times in 2025–26 (Chinese search audit's "circular noise"; TikTok audit at κ = 0.42 with safety-filtered annotators; the three-model judge panel in Overreliance) and has no method for it, while co-signing GUIDE-LLM's demand for psychometric validation. That is a real, documented gap in the specific lab you are applying to, even though it is not a gap in the field.

**Genuinely novel: `False` · Feasible in 3 years: `True`**

**Already done by**

The angle is pre-empted twice over: the statistical half is a solved, packaged problem, and the domain half already has a named benchmark from a strong lab.

Also "Accuracy and Political Bias of News Source Credibility Ratings by Large Language Models", arXiv:2304.00228.

**Strongest objection**

The correction only pays when the judge is good, and for exactly these constructs the judge is bad — so the method collapses into a small human audit with extra steps.

The whole PPI/DSL/control-variate family buys you variance reduction proportional to how well the machine label tracks the gold label. Chaganty et al. got 7-13% cost saving with decent metrics. REFLECT reports judges below 55% accuracy on agentic failure detection and worst of all on evidence verification; the false-success work reports AUROC ceilings of 0.65. Plug a judge that weak into PPI and the estimator's effective sample size approaches the size of the human gold set alone. Asad's answer to "how does judge error propagate" therefore terminates in "it propagates so badly that you must go and label by hand", which is not a three-year research programme, it is a negative result plus an annotation budget. And the audit design the call actually asks for (objective 2: variation across models, tasks, interaction histories, degrees of personalisation and information environments) multiplies cells; each cell needs its own sensitivity and specificity estimate because judge error is not constant across tasks, so the human labelling cost scales with the design, not with the study.

Second blade, and the deeper one: sensitivity/specificity correction and latent class models presuppose a well-defined binary latent truth that the reference standard measures with error. "Evidence laundering", "overconfident synthesis" and "sycophancy" are not that. They are contested constructs with genuine human label variation — see FaithBench's gray-area Benign/Questionable hallucination classes, "Rethinking Ground Truth: Human Label Variation in MLLM Benchmarking" (arXiv:2603.19744), and the TREC 2024 finding that a third human agreed with GPT-4o more than with the second human. If the humans do not converge, there is no error rate to correct toward, and the entire apparatus is misapplied: the problem is construct validity, not estimation. Asad's proposed machinery quietly assumes away the harder question the call is actually asking. A sharp reviewer, or Pierri specifically given he co-authored the Nature Human Behaviour reporting checklist for LLMs in behavioural science, will spot this immediately.

Third, on scope, and this is what sinks it as a spine rather than a chapter. Map the angle onto the call's four objectives. Objective 1 (operationalise the failure modes): partial. Objective 2 (reproducible benchmarks and longitudinal audits): partial. Objective 3 (identify mechanisms in retrieval, ranking, synthesis, memory and reasoning through controlled experiments with open models and modular agent architectures): nothing. Objective 4 (develop and validate lightweight safeguards — source verification, provenance-aware generation, weak-model oversight, model steering, interaction-design interventions): nothing. A proposal spined on the reference-standard problem is silent on half the call and on the entire build-something half. The call wants an auditor who then fixes agents; this angle produces an auditor who audits the auditors and never touches an agent's internals. "A methods paper wearing a costume" is the correct charge, and the costume is thin.

Fourth, a supervision and CV risk. Neither Pierri (computational social science, misinformation, AI search ecosystems) nor Brambilla (data science, web engineering, software modelling) is a statistician; a thesis whose core contribution is an estimator correction leaves Asad the only person in the room who owns the technical spine, on one scholarship, in three years. And it is visibly a re-run of the Haig paper: same move (the instrument is part of the measurement), same method (simulation with known ground truth, sign flips), object swapped from platforms to agents. A reviewer reading his CV will see him proposing to do his existing unpublished paper again. That reads as narrowness, not as depth.

Finally, the ArtemisAI v3-to-v4 story that motivates this — benchmark switched from the 97-post human holdout to Claude's labels at the same time the models changed — is rhetorically excellent but is, technically, the textbook instrumentation threat to internal validity (Campbell & Stanley 1963). Presenting a known design confound as a research contribution invites the reviewer to say so. It works as a motivating anecdote and as a hazard to design against; it does not work as a claim to novelty.

**Verdict**

REFUTED as the spine of the proposal. Keep it as one work package, roughly six to eight months, inside a thesis whose centre of gravity is elsewhere.

Why not novel: the statistical claim is solved (PPI, DSL, control variates, judge sensitivity/specificity correction) and industrialised for agentic evaluation specifically (GLIDE, arXiv:2605.31278). The domain question is owned by a named 2026 benchmark from Yale and IBM (REFLECT, arXiv:2605.19196) that already establishes the premise empirically. The longitudinal calibrated-judge design is already published in PLOS ONE (Wiese, Feb 2026). The judge-swap sensitivity question has at least five 2026 papers on it. Defaulting to genuinely_novel=false is correct here, not cautious.

Why feasible is the wrong question: it is very feasible, and that is the objection. It needs no privileged platform access and no DSA Article 40 data — commercial agents can be queried through public interfaces, and Asad's seven years of collector engineering (68 collectors at M+C Saatchi, 500+ at Dubizzle, web-scraping-guide.com on anti-bot and agentic browsers) makes the harness a genuine comparative advantage over a typical DEIB candidate. But a project that is cheap, needs no access and rests on off-the-shelf statistics is a strong first-year paper, not a three-year thesis. The scarce resource is the human gold set, and that is a budget line, not a research question.

The one crack that survives, stated narrowly and honestly: nobody has re-analysed the published web-agent audits under judge-error correction to see WHICH of their comparative conclusions survive. DeepTRACE audits nine commercial systems on eight epistemic dimensions with a judge validated at Pearson 0.62 on factual support and explicitly does not propagate that error. The concrete unclaimed result is: take the reported between-system gaps in DeepTRACE and its successors, collect a modest human gold set, apply the existing correction, and report how many system rankings and how many "failure rate" headlines are not distinguishable from judge noise. That is a real contribution and a good paper. It borrows the estimator rather than inventing one, which is the right and honest posture. It is not a PhD, and it must not be the answer to "what is your thesis about".

What to do with it instead. Invert the relationship. Make the thesis about the call's objectives 3 and 4 — mechanisms in retrieval, ranking, synthesis and memory using open models and modular architectures, and the safeguards, particularly weak-model oversight, which is the one place in the call where an imperfect judge is native rather than imported. Weak-model oversight IS the reference-standard problem with the sign flipped: the call asks Asad to BUILD a system that relies on a weaker model to check a stronger one, and everything he knows about judge error propagation becomes the reason his safeguard is designed correctly and validated honestly, rather than the thing he is claiming to discover. That framing keeps his real edge (measurement discipline, the generalizability-theory toolkit, the production labelling stack, the collector infrastructure) load-bearing while giving the supervisors the audit-and-mitigate arc the call was written to fund. It also stops the proposal reading as the Haig paper with agents substituted for platforms.

Second, if any version of this angle survives into the proposal, it must cite Egami's DSL and the PPI line explicitly and position against them, because a DEIB committee that includes anyone quantitative will know them, and an angle that presents a known estimator as a discovery is worse than no angle at all.

### ANGLE: Measurement invariance applied to agent-evaluation metrics: does a benchmark score mean the same thing across model versions, across personalisation states, and over time?

**Genuinely novel: `False` · Feasible in 3 years: `True`**

**Already done by**

SCOOPED ON THE CORE CLAIM, TWICE, IN 2026.

(1) ACROSS MODEL VERSIONS — DONE. Jon-Paul Cacioli, "Beyond the Mean: Within-Model Reliable Change Detection for LLM Evaluation" (arXiv 2604.27405, 30 Apr 2026). Imports the Reliable Change Index from clinical psychology, 2,000 MMLU-Pro items, K=10 samples at T=0.7 (80,000 trials), split-half reliability, SE of difference. Llama 3 to 3.1 (+1.6 pts) and Qwen 2.5 to 3 (+2.8 pts). Finding: the aggregate gain is "the net residual of opposing item-level movements" — 34% of analysable items improved and 28% deteriorated (Llama), 47%/39% (Qwen), median |dp| 0.50 and 0.90; greedy single-shot evaluation missed 42% of reliably changed items. That is Asad's version-comparability question, asked and answered, with the measurement-error machinery, five months before this deadline.

(2) ACROSS MODELS/GROUPS VIA DIF — DONE, NINE DAYS AGO. Qiaoyuan Zheng and Yiqu Yang, "Are Near-Tied LLM Rankings Robust to Family-DIF-Guided Benchmark Recomposition?" (arXiv 2609.00482, submitted 31 Aug 2026). Family-label-free spectral approximation to multidimensional IRT, isolates low-DIF items across model families across five benchmarks, owner-disjoint folds, frozen balanced weights, matched-random subtests as controls. Result: full-benchmark and low-DIF rankings correlate at tau-b .900 to .948, yet 30.9 to 47.1% of near-tied model pairs reverse order in four of five benchmarks. This is literally "the score does not mean the same thing across groups, and it flips rankings."

(3) ASAD'S OWN G-THEORY MOVE, ALREADY TRANSFERRED TO LLM EVAL. "Hidden Measurement Error in LLM Pipelines Distorts Annotation, Evaluation, and Benchmarking" (arXiv 2604.11581, Messing). Applies generalizability theory variance decomposition and the decision-study framework to LLM evaluation pipelines, items as object of measurement, prompt variants / judge model / temperature / replications as facets, on Chatbot Arena and MMLU. Naive standard errors are 40 to 60% smaller than the corrected SE; halves MMLU estimation error. This is the "Is the Platform Part of the Measurement?" manoeuvre — G-theory over a mode facet — already executed on LLM evaluation by someone else. Reinforced by "Variance-Aware LLM Annotation for Strategy Research" (2601.02370), explicitly grounded in G-theory, five variance sources, design choices shifting outcomes 12 to 85 percentage points; and "Rating the Raters: Rasch Measurement Theory for LLM Evaluation" (2608.27463), many-facet Rasch over nine LLM annotators covering severity, item calibration, question-order robustness and target-identity sensitivity.

(4) THE FIELD ALREADY HAS A REVIEW THAT NAMES THIS AGENDA. Ye, Jin, Xie, Zhang and Song, "Large Language Model Psychometrics: A Systematic Review of Evaluation, Validation, and Enhancement" (arXiv 2505.08245, v3 11 Mar 2026) explicitly lists measurement invariance and differential item functioning as core properties of the emerging LLM-psychometrics programme. When a systematic review has already named your framing as a sub-area, the contribution is "someone finally ran it", not a new idea. Adjacent occupied ground: "Auditing LLM Benchmarks with Item Response Theory" (2605.30504, 114 models across seven benchmarks); "Lost in Benchmarks? Rethinking LLM Benchmarking with Item Response Theory" (2505.15055, PSN-IRT, 12 LLMs, 11 datasets); "Measuring what Matters: Construct Validity in LLM Benchmarks" (2511.04703).

(5) THE AGENT-SPECIFIC VERSION IS TAKEN TOO, AND BY A BIGGER VARIABLE THAN THE THREE PROPOSED. Harness-Bench, "Measuring Harness Effects across Models in Realistic Agent Workflows" (2605.27922); "Stop Comparing LLM Agents Without Disclosing the Harness" (2605.23950); "'LLM Agent Performance' Is Not a Single Evaluation Target" (2602.03238). Reported magnitudes: the Holistic Agent Leaderboard shows single-model swings of up to ~48 points on SWE-bench Verified Mini across scaffolds; at fixed model, changing harness shifts pass@1 by 8.5 to 13.0 points, while at fixed harness changing the model shifts it 2.5 to 5.0 points; Claude Opus 4.5 on SWE-bench Pro scores 45.9% under SEAL and 55.4% under Claude Code. For agents, the harness moves the score more than the model does. The proposed grouping variables (version, personalisation state, time) omit the one that empirically dominates.

(6) AGENT BENCHMARK VALIDITY, BROADLY. Zhu et al., "Establishing Best Practices for Building Rigorous Agentic Benchmarks" (2507.02825): the 43-item Agentic Benchmark Checklist; 8 of 10 popular agentic benchmarks show severe validity issues; do-nothing agents pass 38% of tau-bench airline tasks; augmented tests change 41% of SWE-bench rankings; misestimation up to 100% in relative terms. Also William Caban, "Measurement Without Validity: The Compounding Reliability Problem in Agentic AI Evaluation" (2608.00794, Aug 2026), which argues agentic evaluation fails construct validity, inter-rater reliability and content validity simultaneously with a multiplicative compounding model across pipeline layers. And the meta-level is taken as well: "Auditing the Audit: Five Failure Modes in Benchmark-Validity Audits" (2607.02586).

(7) THE INVARIANCE FAILURE IS ALREADY DOCUMENTED FOR ONE OF THE CALL'S OWN FAILURE MODES. "What Counts as AI Sycophancy? A Taxonomy and Expert Survey of a Fragmented Construct" (2605.21778): different studies use the same label for behaviours differing in form, mechanism and measurement, and critically, "SycEval and ELEPHANT produce inverted rankings for the same models." That is a documented cross-instrument invariance failure on sycophancy, obtained without a single latent-variable model. Syco-bench reports inter-test correlations r < 0.3 across its four sycophancy subtests. Asad would be re-deriving a known result with heavier machinery.

(8) THE TEMPORAL HALF, IN THE WEB SETTING. "Characterizing Web Search in the Age of Generative AI" (2510.11560) already separates temporal drift (model and web-content change) from stochasticity (run-to-run variation) in generative search, with experiments repeated roughly two months apart.

**Strongest objection**

The single sharpest objection: the angle would be scooped on arrival and mis-specified on the variable that matters most.

Scooped: Cacioli (Apr 2026) already answered "does the score mean the same thing across model versions" with an explicit measurement-error criterion, and Zheng and Yang (31 Aug 2026, nine days before this deadline) already answered "does it mean the same thing across model groups" with IRT-based DIF and showed 30 to 47% of near-tied pairs reverse. Asad would submit on 18 Sep 2026 a three-year plan whose headline finding was published in the preceding five months on cheaper substrates. By the time he has data in year two, the marginal claim is "and it also happens for agents."

Mis-specified: for agents the dominant source of score non-comparability is the harness, not the model version. Harness-Bench and the HAL leaderboard put harness-only swings at 8.5 to 13.0 points and up to ~48 points, against 2.5 to 5.0 points for swapping the model at fixed harness. A proposal that names version, personalisation and time as its grouping variables and does not name the scaffold will read to anyone tracking agent evaluation as a proposal written from the psychometrics literature rather than from the agent literature.

Second objection, on fit. It is a methods paper in a costume, and the tell is precise: nothing in the design requires the agent to be web-connected. The dependent variable is a benchmark score; the unit of analysis is an item; the web appears only as noise to be partialled out. The entire thesis could be run against a frozen offline benchmark and lose nothing except the call's title. A call funding "auditing and mitigating epistemic failures in web-connected AI agents" wants the live, adversarial, drifting web as the object of study, not as a variance component. It also serves at most part of objective 2 and touches none of objective 1 (operationalising evidence laundering, source omission, overconfident synthesis, bias amplification), objective 3 (mechanisms in retrieval, ranking, synthesis, memory), or objective 4 (safeguards: source verification, provenance-aware generation, weak-model oversight, steering). Objective 4 is where a scholarship committee looks for the payoff, and this angle produces no safeguard, only a warning.

Third objection, on the supervisors. Francesco Pierri is a computational social scientist who audits deployed web systems: misinformation exposure across search engines, LLMs and AI Overviews; overreliance on AI in information-seeking from video (EMNLP 2026); conspiracy-mindset probing (EMNLP 2026 Findings, ACL 2026); persona assignment effects on stereotypes and safeguards (ACM TIST); BiasGym; three ICWSM 2026 papers; "The Role of AI Agents for Online Information Disorders". Marco Brambilla leads the Data Science Lab on explainable AI, KG-plus-RAG, data extraction and scraping, and social media analytics. Neither publishes latent-variable measurement modelling. There is no one in DEIB running MG-CFA, IRT or G-theory, so the proposal's core method would have no local supervisor and would be assessed by two people who do not practise it. Note also that Pierri co-authored "A reporting checklist for large language models in behavioural science" (Nature Human Behaviour) — the measurement-hygiene niche in his own group is already filled by a checklist, which lowers the marginal value of a three-year invariance thesis.

Fourth objection, and the quiet one: objective 2 of the call already says the benchmarks must measure "how failures vary across models, tasks, interaction histories, degrees of personalisation and information environments." Proposing to test whether scores are comparable across models, personalisation states and time is restating the call's own objective in psychometric vocabulary. Reviewers notice when a proposal's contribution is their own sentence with better nouns.

Fifth, on feasibility, which is not the binding constraint but is not clean either. Access is a non-issue: this needs API calls, not DSA Article 40 or platform data, so the usual access objection does not bite. What bites is (a) cost and latency — a static QA item costs a fraction of a cent and a second, whereas an agentic web rollout costs minutes and dollars with browsing, retries and a judge, so Cacioli's 80,000-trial design is trivial on MMLU-Pro and financially serious on agents; realistic scale on a Polimi scholarship is hundreds of items, which is thin for MG-CFA and marginal for many-group DIF; (b) local independence, which both IRT and CFA require, is violated by construction inside an agent trajectory, where items share retrieved context and memory, and there is no cheap fix a reviewer will accept; (c) anchor decay — closed model versions are deprecated on the provider's schedule, typically inside three years, so a 2026 to 2029 longitudinal invariance design can lose its anchor form mid-study, which is exactly the failure the equating literature warns about when the assessment evolves or anchor information becomes unavailable; (d) the live web is a non-stationary population he does not control, so "invariance over time" confounds model change, retrieval-index change and content change, and disentangling them requires held-fixed corpora, at which point the study stops being web-connected and returns to objection two.

**Verdict**

REFUTED as stated. Do not build the proposal on this angle. The claim "benchmark scores may not be comparable across versions, personalisation states and time" is not a hypothesis in September 2026, it is a documented finding, established in the preceding five months by Cacioli (2604.27405), Zheng and Yang (2609.00482), Messing (2604.11581), the Rasch and G-theory annotation papers, the Agentic Benchmark Checklist, and the harness literature. Asad's differentiator would have to be the psychometric vocabulary itself, and the systematic review 2505.08245 has already installed that vocabulary in the field. Writing this proposal means asking two computational social scientists to fund three years of work whose headline result is on arXiv and whose method has no supervisor in their department.

WHAT ACTUALLY SURVIVES, AND IT IS NOT SMALL. One grouping variable in the call's objective 2 is genuinely unoccupied: the information environment. Everyone above varies the model, the judge, the prompt, the persona or the harness, because those are free. Nobody varies the retrieved evidence set under experimental control, because doing that requires building and holding a corpus of web content with known provenance, known source-quality distribution, known contamination, and the ability to serve it to an agent as if it were live. That is web-collection engineering: 68 collectors across 10 platforms with an automated repair layer, 500-plus collectors across 15 countries, and published reference work on anti-bot systems, detection anatomy and agentic browsers. It is precisely the capability a psychometrician does not have and precisely the capability Pierri's audit work needs. It also sits inside the call's objective 3 (controlled experiments with open models and modular agent architectures) rather than dangling off objective 2.

The reframing to test in the next round: the contribution is constructing controlled, provenance-labelled information environments and measuring how an agent's epistemic failures respond when the evidence distribution is manipulated while the model, harness and prompt are held fixed. Invariance and DIF then become instruments used inside that design to show that a failure metric does not survive the environment swap, not the thesis itself. Two guardrails if he goes that way: name the harness as a controlled factor from page one, because the literature says it dominates, and make sure at least one chapter delivers an objective-4 safeguard (source verification or provenance-aware generation evaluated in those environments), because a thesis that only reports that measurement is hard will not win the single scholarship.

One more asset worth using in the honest framing rather than hiding: his own v1-v3 versus v4-v5 benchmark switch at ArtemisAI, where models were scored against a 97-post human holdout and then against Claude's labels, is a real, first-hand instance of the reference moving underneath a reported accuracy gain of 64.7 to 85.8. ### ANGLE: Longitudinal drift in agent epistemic behaviour: a shadow-anchored bridging design that keeps an audit series comparable across silent model updates.

**Genuinely novel: `False` · Feasible in 3 years: `False`**

**Already done by**

Four papers land on this directly, three of them from 2026.

1. Habba et al., "Growing Pains: Extensible and Efficient LLM Benchmarking Via Fixed Parameter Calibration" (arXiv 2604.12843, 14 Apr 2026). Multidimensional IRT (2PL) with fixed-parameter calibration. Anchor item parameters are locked, new items are calibrated onto the locked scale, and the stated purpose is that "results collected at different times can be compared directly". They report 2 to 3 percentage point prediction error from 100 anchor questions, Spearman rho >= 0.9 on rankings. This is the bridging design, published, five months before the PoliMi deadline. Its gaps are English knowledge/reasoning MCQ only, no agents, no retrieval, and it explicitly does not solve anchor contamination or saturation over time.

2. Anon./Yitao Li, "Who Drifted: the System or the Judge? Anytime-Valid Attribution in LLM Evaluation Pipelines" (arXiv 2606.15474, Jun 2026). Sequential, anytime-valid attribution of an observed score change to the measured system versus the measuring instrument. This is the identification problem a shadow anchor is supposed to solve, solved with stronger statistics (optional stopping, valid at every look) than a fixed anchor set gives you.

3. "Same Agent, Different Answers: A Repeat-Aware Audit of Corpus-Induced Answer Churn in Retrieval-Augmented QA" (arXiv 2608.22856, Aug 2026). This is the closest hit and it is one month old. The Snapshot Compatibility Audit holds model identifier, prompt, retrieval policy, evidence depth, rendering and generation controls fixed, varies only the corpus (frozen FineWeb prefix expanded from one to seven shards), and estimates "excess churn" by subtracting same-snapshot repeat disagreement from cross-snapshot disagreement. That subtraction is exactly the shadow-anchor logic. Findings: 6.44 pp normalised-exact and 10.25 pp semantic excess churn on Natural Questions while exact-match accuracy moved only -1.50 pp, and 40/400 questions flipped semantically under a stable aggregate.

4. "The Moving Target: A Longitudinal Audit of Trustworthiness Drift Across Twelve Checkpoints of Open-Source Chat LLMs" (arXiv 2607.02587, Jul 2026). Fixed 200-item basket, three prompt templates, four release lines (Yi, Qwen, Mistral, Gemma), TruthfulQA/BBQ/ToxiGen/CrowS-Pairs/XSTest, with drop-one robustness checks. Its conclusion is actively hostile to the proposed angle: scores should not be carried forward across versions, they should be re-measured on each materially new release. A referee can cite this to say the bridging premise is the wrong response to drift.

Also close:
- Wiese, "Human-anchored longitudinal comparison of generative AI with a bias-calibrated LLM-as-judge", PLOS ONE, 2 Feb 2026. Ten weekly waves, fixed prompt bank of 240 items held constant across all waves, plus 30 human-anchored prompts sampled weekly to bias-correct the LLM judge via Bradley-Terry, across six domains including factual QA and safety. That is a shadow-anchored longitudinal series with a human anchor, already run.
- Chen, Zaharia and Zou, arXiv 2307.09009 / Harvard Data Science Review, founded the genre. Narayanan and Kapoor's rebuttal (AI Snake Oil, 19 Jul 2023) is the standing critique that this genre confuses behaviour change with capability change, and any new entrant inherits that critique.
- NewsGuard AI False Claims Monitor, running since July 2024. Ten false-claim fingerprints x three personas (innocent, leading, malign) = 30 prompts per model, roughly eleven consumer chatbots, published trend series: 18% false-claim repetition Aug 2024, 35% Aug 2025, 28.79% Jan 2026. A non-academic actor already owns the public longitudinal epistemic-failure trend line, and pointedly refreshes items each wave rather than anchoring them.
- LiveBench (ICLR 2025 spotlight, arXiv 2406.19314): the field's chosen answer to item staleness is monthly refresh, not anchoring. Argue against that, do not ignore it.
- Nautilus Compass (arXiv 2605.09863): black-box persona drift detection for production LLM agents, i.e. change-point detection of unannounced updates already exists.
- WebCanvas / Mind2Web-Live (arXiv 2406.12373, ICML 2024): the web-agent community's existing answer to environment drift, key-node scoring plus a community-maintained live dataset, motivated by 96 of 780 Mind2Web tasks (12%) being dead on the live web.
- Psychometric core is 40 years old: common-item non-equivalent groups (NEAT) linking, item parameter drift, anchor purification, and NAEP bridge studies. Nothing in "shadow-anchored bridging" is new measurement theory, it is transfer.
- Industry MLOps already ships this vocabulary: frozen held-out anchor sets with frozen labels excluded from calibration, shadow traffic, canary prompts, golden sets. "Shadow anchor" will not read as a coinage to an engineering reviewer.

**Strongest objection**

The design is not identified for web-connected agents, and no amount of anchoring fixes it.

Psychometric linking works because the anchor item is literally the same item across administrations. Only the population moves. In an agent audit, three things move at once: the model weights and system prompt, the retrieval and ranking stack, and the live web corpus itself. A shadow anchor can hold the *prompt* constant, but the *item* is a query whose answer-bearing evidence is fetched fresh. So the anchor cannot separate "the agent became more sycophantic" from "the top ten documents for that query changed this month". Anchor purification cannot rescue it either: purification assumes drift is item-specific and that most anchors are stable, whereas here the drift is a common cause arriving through the shared index and hitting every anchor simultaneously. The only two escapes are (a) freeze the corpus, which is precisely arXiv 2608.22856 and stops being a web audit, or (b) run a parallel non-agent measurement of the web environment as a control series, which doubles the cost and is itself unstable.

Three supporting objections:

1. The noise floor is the same size as the signal. arXiv 2608.22856 found 6 to 10 pp of churn under a 1.5 pp accuracy change. Serving nondeterminism means same prompt, same model, same day gives different answers, and if the epistemic construct is scored by an LLM judge, the instrument drifts too (arXiv 2606.15474). After you subtract the repeat baseline, the per-wave N needed to detect a meaningful shift in sycophancy or source diversity, per model, per topic, is large, and every wave costs money.

2. The bridge has no observable administration boundary. Bridging designs need to know when the form changed. Consumer surfaces (ChatGPT, Gemini app, Perplexity, AI Overviews, Copilot), which are the only surfaces where "web-connected agent" is a real object, offer no version pinning, no changelog, and no stable identifier. You would be estimating the change-point and the linking constants from the same data. Weakly identified at best. And on the API side, pinning does not save you: Anthropic warns dateless aliases silently repoint and even pinned IDs shift with infrastructure changes.

3. The panel dies before the thesis does. OpenAI gives roughly six months' notice for GA models, three for specialised variants, two weeks for previews; Anthropic sixty days; Google two weeks for previews. Model lifecycles are now six to twelve months. Over a 1 Nov 2026 to 2029 window the system panel turns over at least twice, so most of the series you are trying to link will be gone, and the linking is worth least exactly where the discontinuity is largest.

Fourth, on scope, and this is the one that costs the scholarship. The call has four objectives. This angle touches the back half of objective 2 only, and touches the plumbing of it rather than the epistemic content. It says nothing about objective 1 (which failure modes, operationalised), objective 3 (mechanisms in retrieval, ranking, synthesis, memory, reasoning via controlled experiments on open models), or objective 4 (safeguards: source verification, provenance-aware generation, weak-model oversight, steering). Objective 4 is the mitigation half of the call title and is where the panel's intervention story lives. Pierri works on misinformation, AI search engine audits and human-AI interaction, and has already published a cross-system audit of search engines, LLMs and AI Overviews. Brambilla runs a data science lab on explainable AI, web data extraction, social media analytics and knowledge graphs. Neither publishes equating or IRT linking. Their venues (ICWSM, WebSci, CHI, WWW) reward a finding about the web, not a linking constant. So yes, it reads as a methods paper in a costume, and specifically it reads as Asad's *existing* methods paper in a costume: the Haig paper already applies generalizability theory, measurement invariance, DIF and score linking to machine-labelled text. Proposing linking again on a new object invites the supervisor to conclude he has one technique and is shopping for surfaces. That is the most damaging thing a reader can think, and this framing hands it to them.

**Verdict**

Refuted as stated. Do not submit this as the headline angle.

Not novel: the bridging-with-locked-anchors design is Habba et al. (arXiv 2604.12843, Apr 2026); the repeat-baseline subtraction that makes the "shadow" part work is the Snapshot Compatibility Audit (arXiv 2608.22856, Aug 2026); drift attribution between system and instrument is arXiv 2606.15474 (Jun 2026); the human-anchored weekly longitudinal series with a frozen prompt bank is Wiese, PLOS ONE (Feb 2026); the fixed-basket longitudinal trustworthiness audit is arXiv 2607.02587 (Jul 2026), which concludes you should re-measure rather than link. The underlying psychometrics is NEAT linking and item parameter drift, standard since the late 1980s. There is no defensible novelty claim left, only a claim of transfer to agents, and the agent transfer is where the design breaks.

Not feasible as specified: running the series is feasible without DSA Article 40 or any platform access, since black-box query auditing needs neither, and the Ulloa/Makhortykh/Urman virtual-agent methodology is public and proven at scale. But feasible-to-run is not feasible-to-conclude. The comparability guarantee is not identifiable from black-box data when model, index and corpus move together; the change-points are unobserved on exactly the consumer surfaces that matter; and the system panel will not survive three years of deprecation calendars. Asad would spend three years maintaining an instrument and end with a methods note.

What survives, if he wants to keep the territory (offered as raw material for him to write, not as prose):

1. The honest version is an empirical claim, not a design claim: that published agent-audit findings do not replicate across silent updates, and that an observed audit delta decomposes into model change, index change, corpus change, and run-to-run variance in shares nobody has measured on the live web. That is a finding about the epistemic status of the audit literature, it sits squarely inside objective 2, and 2608.22856 gives him the estimator and the frozen-corpus baseline to extend rather than reinvent.

2. The stronger inversion is to anchor the environment instead of the model. Build a controlled information environment with planted documents, manipulated provenance chains and known ground-truth source distributions, so the item genuinely is constant and the agent is the only moving part. That is objective 3 verbatim ("controlled experiments with open models and modular agent architectures"), it makes evidence laundering and source omission directly measurable rather than inferred, it needs no platform access, and it opens a clean path into objective 4 safeguards. His seven years of production collection work (68 collectors, automated repair layers, anti-bot and agentic-browser expertise, web-scraping-guide.com) is a real advantage for standing up and maintaining that environment, whereas it is only decoration in a linking-constants proposal.

3. Keep the measurement literacy as a weapon inside the proposal, not as its thesis. The single most persuasive thing he can say is the one from his own ArtemisAI stack: v1 to v3 were scored against a 97-post human holdout, v4 to v5 against Claude's labels, so part of the 64.7 to 85.8 rise is the benchmark moving under the model. That is a first-person, verifiable illustration of the exact failure the call cares about, and it earns the methods point in one paragraph without turning the whole proposal into psychometrics.

## 9. WHAT NOT TO CLAIM — HARD LIST

1. **Do not claim the psychometric framing is new.** arXiv:2505.08245 named measurement invariance and DIF as sub-areas of an existing programme in March 2026.
2. **Do not claim that showing benchmark scores are non-comparable across versions or groups is a contribution.** Cacioli (2604.27405) and Zheng & Yang (2609.00482) published it in the five months before the deadline.
3. **Do not claim G-theory applied to LLM evaluation as your idea.** Messing (2604.11581) did it, on Chatbot Arena and MMLU, with the naive-SE-is-40-to-60%-too-small result.
4. **Do not claim that correcting for LLM-judge error is novel.** PPI, PPI++, DSL, AutoEval Done Right, the bias-corrected judge estimator, and GLIDE — a shipping stratified-PPI library for *agentic* evaluation — are all prior art, on top of forty years of imperfect-gold-standard epidemiology.
5. **Do not claim to be first to show LLM judges are unreliable on evidence quality.** REFLECT (2605.19196) reports best judges below 55% and worst on evidence verification, expert-validated at κ = 0.86.
6. **Do not claim evidence laundering has not been measured.** Allaham & Diakopoulos (2605.23684) found ~16% AI-generated sources across four generative search engines over 712 queries.
7. **Do not claim sycophancy instruments have never been shown to disagree.** SycEval and ELEPHANT produce inverted rankings for the same models (2605.21778); Syco-bench subtests correlate at r < 0.3.
8. **Do not claim you have published anything.** You have not. The methods paper with Dr Ella Haig is finished and unposted. Say "in preparation" or post it to arXiv first.
9. **Do not claim experience fine-tuning generative LLMs.** You have fine-tuned encoders. Different competence, and one the panel distinguishes.
10. **Do not claim experience with conversational, multi-turn, or agentic systems.** ArtemisAI is a labelling pipeline with confidence routing. Calling it an agent is the claim most likely to be caught and most damaging when it is.
11. **Do not claim human-subjects, IRB, or Prolific experience.** None.
12. **Do not claim platform data access.** You have collection capability, which is a different and in some ways better thing. Say the true one.
13. **Do not quantify how much of ArtemisAI's 64.7 → 85.8 gain is the benchmark moving.** You never scored v4/v5 against the human holdout, so the counterfactual does not exist.
14. **Do not generalise the wrong-sign result beyond its setting.** It is machine-labelled social text across four platforms in simulation, not agents on the live web.
15. **Do not propose anything that depends on DSA Article 40 data.** Pierri's own CACM piece describes the process as slow, adversarial and under-resourced, with first decisions only expected in late Feb 2026.
16. **Do not propose a design that omits the harness as a controlled factor.** The scaffold moves agent scores more than the model does; omitting it is the fastest way to look unread.
17. **Do not propose an LLM-agent simulation of users without addressing under-dispersion.** Composta, Fontana, Corso and Pierri already found LLM agents show less heterogeneity in tone and toxicity than real data and are insensitive to parameter variation.
18. **Do not promise a thesis whose only output is a warning that measurement is hard.** Objective 4 exists, it is where the payoff is, and there is one scholarship.
19. **Do not describe network science, opinion dynamics or epidemic modelling as competencies.** They are in the lab's record, not yours.
20. **Do not claim a mechanism you can only infer from behaviour.** Without weights access, objective 3's "mechanisms in retrieval, ranking, synthesis, memory and reasoning" must be phrased as behavioural inference under controlled inputs — which is Pierri's own native method, so this costs you nothing to say honestly.
