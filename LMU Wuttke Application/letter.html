Your work. AI Conversational Interviewing (Wuttke, Lang, Klamm, Würschinger and Kreuter, 2026) shows what a battery from the German Longitudinal Election Study cannot: not only where partisans stand on migration, but what comes to mind first, with FDP supporters opening on the labour market and Die Linke supporters on humanitarian concerns. The paper is also candid about two things I recognise. Voice interviews produce longer, more conversational answers and text interviews shorter, denser ones, with chat replies shrinking after about the tenth turn, so the mode comparisons describe systems as deployed rather than a pure modality effect. The first is a measurement problem and the second a pipeline problem, and I have worked on both.

A question I could contribute to. Before belief systems are compared across modes and across your thirteen countries, are the topic and frame labels behind them invariant? If voice produces looser, longer reasoning and chat shorter, denser claims, a frame such as “causal reasoning” may be coded more often in one mode for the same underlying view, and a democratic attitude may look well-founded or superficial partly because of the interviewer system. I would treat mode, language and country as facets, test the coding scheme for invariance and differential item functioning against human-coded subsamples, and only then compare. On the engineering side I would make linkage and turn-level logging automatic, so that no interview is lost to a typed ID. I have built text pipelines in English, Arabic, Urdu and Hindi, and I know how differently the same stance can read across languages.
