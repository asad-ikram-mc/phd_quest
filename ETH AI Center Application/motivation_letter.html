Dear Members of the Selection Committee,

When an LLM says Reddit is angrier than X about the same budget, three things could be true: the people differ, the instrument differs, or the platform chose what we saw. My MSc system, which read public reaction to UK economic policy on X, Reddit, YouTube and Quora, assumed the answer. When I looked closely, it treated the four platforms as if they measured alike, silently dropped comments written in Urdu or Hindi, and looked accurate only because it agreed with the lexicon that had made its training labels. My research interest is one question: when AI reads what the public thinks, is it right, and equally right on every platform and in every language? Professor Ash's group asks when LLMs can measure social-science constructs; Professor Mendler-Dünner asks how much a platform shapes what people see. My doctorate would sit where those questions meet. It would make the instruments that now read the public what the ETH AI Center calls “trustworthy, accessible, and inclusive AI systems”: checked against people, open to audit, and fair to Urdu and Roman Urdu.

Research area: is the platform part of the measurement? My project would separate those three sources. It builds on my preprint with Dr Ella Haig (Ikram and Haig 2026, arXiv:2610.16757), which adapts generalizability theory, measurement invariance and score linking to machine-labelled sentiment, treating the platform like a survey mode. Its known-truth simulation shows that pooling platforms misleads when they measure differently and the mix shifts, and that relative linking corrects this when the true measurement functions are known; with a realistic reference point the advantage shrinks, so how much survives estimation is what the doctorate must show. Study 1 tests the instrument: does an LLM give the same stance and sentiment to the same political message in Urdu script, Roman Urdu and English? Each LLM annotator is treated as a test item and each script as a group (differential item functioning), against disagreement-aware labels from native-speaker coders, with the message held fixed. Study 2 holds the story fixed instead: the same story from the same outlet on YouTube and Facebook. LLM error is measured against human labels on each platform, and the comments a platform shows first are compared with all comments, so a cross-platform gap splits into instrument error, platform selection and what remains, audience and expression. A small, consented donation pilot of what users were actually shown is a stretch goal, to be designed with Prof. Mendler-Dünner. Study 3 asks what correction costs: how many human labels design-based correction (Egami et al. 2023) needs before a cross-platform or cross-language comparison can be trusted, and whether correcting changes a substantive conclusion. A null result would support comparisons researchers already make. Study 1 needs no platform data; for Study 2 I would apply to the YouTube Researcher Program and the Meta Content Library, and if access fails, Study 1 and YouTube alone still answer the core question. Model and prompt choices are known to change LLM-based conclusions (Baumann et al. 2025; Messing 2026), equivalence across languages has to be validated rather than assumed (Lind et al. 2025), and the platform has been named as an error source (Sen et al. 2021). What these leave open is whether LLM labels mean the same across South Asian scripts and across platforms when the content is held fixed. I have not found a Roman Urdu political-stance gold set; the project would build one.

Why it needs both mentors. Prof. Ash's group defines what is being measured: how LLMs can measure scalar social-science constructs (Licht et al., EMNLP 2025), whether they can capture human annotator disagreement (Ni et al., EACL 2026), and point-in-time LLMs, which bear on whether opinion changed or the model did. With him I would fix the construct and the estimand and run Study 1. Prof. Mendler-Dünner's work supplies the other half. A ranking system is performative: the comments it shows first can change which comments get replies and likes, so the opinion mix we collect is partly produced by the platform. Her measurement of a search engine's performative power (Mendler-Dünner, Carovano and Hardt, NeurIPS 2024) is the model for Study 2, and her finding that LLM survey answers carry ordering and labelling biases (Dominguez-Olmedo, Hardt and Mendler-Dünner, NeurIPS 2024) is the warning for Study 1. An instrument study that ignores the platform is incomplete; a platform audit with an unvalidated instrument measures nothing.

Competence for Trustworthy AI and LLMs. In my MSc (Distinction, including the Explainable AI module) I compared a VADER lexicon baseline, a BiLSTM and a fine-tuned BERT, used temperature-scaling calibration, compared models with McNemar and bootstrap tests, and explained predictions with LIME and integrated gradients. The preprint adds DIF tests, a multilevel model and a 500-replication known-truth simulation, with public code. At M+C Saatchi Fluency (since December 2024) I apply the same discipline to LLM labelling. In a twelve-month consumer discourse study for the New York team, people signed off an 18-theme codebook, a 500-row hand-checked pilot compared three prompt versions, and every GPT-4o-mini label carries a prompt-version hash, because the prompt is part of the instrument. I also built Ford Crawler IQ, where AI agents repair and build more than 60 forum crawlers and changes ship only after verification. As co-founder and CTO of Artemis AI (since February 2025) I lead the architecture and evaluation of a social-media intelligence system with 48 models in the architecture, about ten running weekly over 3.39 million public comments, read-only through the official Meta Graph API. Our labelling cascade passes comments from our own models to an LLM when confidence is low, and our tracker had to call model agreement “an accuracy proxy”: when one model becomes the benchmark for another, a rising score can mean agreement with that model rather than with people (Kim et al. 2025).

Strengths for my impact areas, Foundations of Data Science and Apertus / Open Source LLMs. I am a native Urdu speaker and have worked with public text in English, Arabic, Urdu and Hindi, so I can design the Urdu and Roman Urdu codebook and gold set with native-speaker coders, not at second hand. My dissertation measured public reaction to UK economic policy across four platforms, and my preprint brings survey methodology and psychometrics to machine-labelled data. An open, frozen multilingual model such as Apertus is the auditable annotator this design needs: because it does not change silently, a shift in labels can be traced to opinion rather than to the instrument, and its labelling of Urdu script and Roman Urdu could be traced back to the crawl it was trained on.
