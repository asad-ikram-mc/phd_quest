Why I Am Fit For This

The field's usual bottleneck, collecting platform data at scale, is my day job. The field's open question, whether those measurements mean the same thing across platforms, is my research.

1 · I build the instruments

Six years inside the industry this research audits

  • M+C Saatchi Fluency (Dec 2024 - present): I designed and operate the multi-platform social listening pipeline behind campaign intelligence for UK Government departments, Amazon, Ford, Nike and Reckitt: Sprinklr and Brandwatch feeds plus custom crawlers into AWS, with SageMaker NLP sentiment models. Senior government communications teams read these reports. I run the Ford LENS fleet: 68 crawlers across 62 domains with a self-healing agent, CI quality gates and KL-divergence drift alarms.
  • Dubizzle Labs (2022 - 2024): led a 3-engineer team owning 500+ regional scrapers across 15 countries in four languages; redesigned the layered warehouse, throughput up 60%; promoted twice in two years.
  • Fix.com (2023 - present): Scrapy pipelines processing 50M+ data points from 200+ sources; Great Expectations validation cut bad production data 70%.
  • Freelance since 2018: 300+ five-star data projects across 400+ websites; author of web-scraping-guide.com, a free public reference covering 86 tools and the legal, ethical and anti-bot landscape.

Why it matters for a PhD: collection risk, the usual killer of platform research projects, is removed. Any data a design needs, I can get, at scale, under the platform's rules.

2 · I did the research

From a Distinction dissertation to a finished paper

  • MSc dissertation (Portsmouth 2025, Distinction, supervisor Dr Ella Haig): cross-platform NLP framework on ~279,000 posts from Twitter/X, Reddit, YouTube and Quora on UK economic policy. Fine-tuned BERT at macro-F1 0.878 against BiLSTM and lexicon baselines, human validation, McNemar and bootstrap testing, NRC emotion analysis, LIME explainability, Prophet forecasting. Central finding: the same policy event produces structurally different sentiment profiles per platform.
  • The methods paper (with Dr Ella Haig, arXiv imminent): "Is the Platform Part of the Measurement?" treats each platform as a survey mode and specifies a six-step psychometric equivalence protocol. Across 500 known-truth replications, a pooled index reports the wrong sign of aggregate sentiment on about a quarter of days; a measurement-adjusted index does not. 23 pages, 12 figures, reviewed by an ICWSM-experienced researcher. Project brief ↗
  • Next: the four-platform empirical application is pre-specified in the paper as the follow-up study; ICWSM 2027 is the target venue for the applied work.

Why it matters: I arrive with publication momentum, not a promise of it. The first paper of the PhD is written before day one.

3 · I live the problem

ArtemisAI: the research question as a commercial reality

I am founder and CTO of ArtemisAI Ltd, a UK social media intelligence platform (launching late 2026): a six-model NLP suite with a three-tier LLM fallback cascade, 3.39M comments ingested on a two-year Meta Graph API backfill, CLIP multimodal fusion, OCR and video-intelligence pipelines gated by CI quality bars and drift alarms, and an LLM analysis layer. In production, our two model tiers agree on 82.9% of sentiment labels and 0.0% of toxicity labels while the toxicity model reports 96.7% average confidence: the paper's phenomenon, live in my own tables. Its honest commercial problem is exactly the research gap: we pool platform scores every day and cannot say what the pooled number measures. No vendor in the industry documents cross-platform equivalence testing. I know because I have read their documentation and built their competitors' pipelines.

Playbook

The Master Email Pattern · v2

Every outreach email follows this. Gold standards: the Bail and Törnberg emails. v2 (25 Aug 2026) upgrades every draft from "dissertation finding" to "finished paper", and folds in the lessons from the Nelimarkka and Ella exchanges. Full version with the worked Bail example: Master_PhD_Email_Pattern.md ↗

The six checks, before any send
1Does the email name the professor's own theory, framework or specific paper?
2Does the dissertation finding land inside that theory?
3Is ArtemisAI's commercial gap expressed as the professor's unsolved research problem?
4Does the bridging question use the professor's exact vocabulary?
5The Nelimarkka test: would the claim survive "the fact that platforms differ is not novel"? Start where their literature stops, and show you read what they cite.
6The AI test: read it aloud. No em-dashes, no dramatic fragments, contractions where natural. If it sounds like a model, redraft.
The structure, eight parts
1 OPEN "I am writing to enquire about PhD opportunities at [inst] for [year] entry" + hook on THEIR named paper, their core claim in their words 2 WHY Chevening built around a specific professional problem: government decisions rest on pipelines that cannot [the failure THEIR work names] 3 PROOF Six years building those pipelines: Fluency (Sprinklr for UK Gov + Amazon, Ford, Nike, Reckitt) · Dubizzle (500+ scrapers, MENA) · Fix.com (50M points, 200+ sources) 4a DISS 279,000 posts · four platforms · BERT macro-F1 0.878 · finding: same event, structurally different profiles per platform 4b PAPER "Is the Platform Part of the Measurement?" with Dr Ella Haig: six-step equivalence protocol + 500-replication simulation; pooled index gets the SIGN wrong ~1/4 of days; adjusted index does not. 23pp · arXiv [link once live] 5 GAP ArtemisAI: their theoretical problem is my operational one - we cannot say what our pooled scores measure 6 BRIDGE The doctoral question, restated in THEIR exact terminology 7 FUND "I am looking for a fully funded position for [year] entry" 8 ASK Fit? Worth a formal application? CV attached; paper, dissertation or proposal on request

Numbers never mix: 279k / 0.878 / four platforms belong to the dissertation; 500 replications / wrong sign a quarter of days / off by an eighth / 23 pages belong to the paper.

The escalations
✉Follow-up = two sentences + one fact that changed (the paper posted, a result, an acceptance). Never a bare nudge.
♡Warm reply, no opening = thank, name the window they mentioned, give the one thing that will have changed by then (the arXiv paper), stop.
⚠Critique reply (the Nelimarkka lesson) = do the homework first, quote their words back and answer under them, read what they cite, move the claim one step past their literature.
◆Pitch page (high stakes only) = a one-page site answering their critique with the literature map, the paper's stats and a work-package plan. Worked example: asadfix.github.io/helsinki_proposal
⌚Late reply (the Ella lesson) = apologise once, real reason in two plain sentences, then show the finished work. The work is the apology.
