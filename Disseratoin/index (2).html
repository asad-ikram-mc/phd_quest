A paper from my MSc dissertation

How much of public sentiment is really the public, and how much is just the platform?

Lots of studies show sentiment differs across Twitter, Reddit, YouTube and Quora, then stop. My contribution is to treat each platform as a measuring instrument, so I can separate how much of that difference is the platform itself from how much is real disagreement, and put the platforms on one comparable scale. As far as I can find, that has not been done for machine-labelled sentiment.

What is genuinely new

My contribution the novelty

Plenty of studies show sentiment differs across platforms, then stop. I treat each platform as a measuring instrument, the way a survey researcher treats phone, online and face to face as different ways of asking the same question.

Survey research (known)

Survey mode

Phone, online, face to face. The same person answers differently depending on how you ask.

→
My version

Social platform

Twitter, Reddit, YouTube, Quora. The same opinion comes out differently on each one.

Here is what I add. As far as I can find, these four things have not been brought together for sentiment that a model has labelled across several platforms:

1
A new lens. Seeing each platform as the measuring instrument turns "platforms differ" from a finding you just report into a measurement problem you can actually solve. the reframe
2
A number. How much of the gap between platforms is the platform itself, and how much is people genuinely disagreeing. Generalizability theory and the multitrait-multimethod approach give a measured split, not a hand-wave. main result
3
A fairness check. A test of which platform comparisons are sound before you trust them, using measurement invariance and DIF. A check others can reuse, not a one-off. reusable
4
A correction. Shared policy events as anchors to put every platform on one comparable scale, so the signals can finally be pooled. first step

To be clear about what is mine: the split between how it is measured and who posts is not, survey work and Sen et al. drew that first, and others have treated human annotators as instruments in the same spirit (Sachdeva et al.). The new part is bringing these tools to the platform itself, for machine-labelled sentiment across platforms. I am not claiming to recover a "true" opinion, only a comparable one.

How the contribution is made

The method, step by step

Four steps, from a raw and incomparable mix to one signal you can compare. Each step shows the closest existing work and what it missed, which is the gap that step fills. The names link to the papers.

Raw sentiment from four platforms, on scales you cannot compare
1
Measure how much of the difference is the platform, not real disagreement main resultgeneralizability theoryClosest: O'Connor et al. 2010. Treated sentiment as noisy and assumed it averages out, so the systematic platform part was never measured.
2
Check which platform comparisons are actually fair newmeasurement invariance, DIFClosest: Boyd et al. 2024. Ran this exact check across platforms, but for a survey questionnaire, not text a model had labelled.
3
Allow for the fact that platforms talk about different topicsmultilevel modelling, topic mix held constantClosest: Jungherr et al. 2017. Tackled who posts; separating the platform's own effect from the topics it happens to discuss was left open.
4
Line up the platforms onto one shared scale first stepequating, with shared policy events as anchorsClosest: Han & Anderson 2026. Corrected platform bias too, but on human star ratings with a survey to check against, not machine-labelled text.
One signal you can compare across platforms
Why the platform part is the hard part

Two kinds of bias, not one

There are two reasons social media can mislead you. The first is well studied. The second, the one I work on, is mostly ignored for sentiment pulled from text, and you cannot fix it the same way.

Already studied

Who posts

The wrong people post, so the crowd is not representative. You fix it by re-weighting to match the population.

Jungherr et al. 2017 ↗
What I work on

How it is measured

Even when the opinion is the same, each platform records it differently. Re-weighting cannot fix that. You have to measure it, and ideally correct it, before mixing the numbers.

Honest limits

What this does not do

I would rather be upfront about the edges. These are the things a careful reviewer will ask about.
  • Sentiment is not the same as support. I measure the mood in the text, which stands in for opinion. I keep that distinction clear.
  • It is observational. I can allow for different topics, but fully separating the platform from who-posts needs the same people on each platform, which is later work.
  • It is these four platforms. The findings are about them, not every platform.
  • The fix needs a real test. Lining the platforms up is step one. Checking it against a UK survey would come later, but the work does not depend on it.

"Most work stops at 'platforms differ.' Mine measures how much of that is the platform rather than real disagreement, then puts the platforms on one scale, so social media can be read as a single comparable signal."
