Profile

At Dubizzle Labs, leading a team that collected text in four languages across 15 countries, I saw that people write differently on forums and on social platforms, yet their text was treated as comparable. As a Chevening Scholar I took that problem to the University of Portsmouth, into an MSc dissertation and a methods manuscript with Dr Ella Haig, then into client analysis at M+C Saatchi Fluency and into ArtemisAI, the company I founded, where I found my own system's accuracy figure rising partly because its reference had moved. I now want to study when people believe the numbers and outputs AI systems produce, which would also shape how ArtemisAI shows confidence to its users.

Fit with the Everyday Media Lab
Validating automated coding against human coders is how I would build stimuli of known accuracy and manipulation checks. McNemar tests, bootstrap intervals and simulation with known ground truth carry over to experimental data, and my Python pipelines could build a simulated professional feed for studying incidental learning.
Research interests
Credibility judgments of AI-generated information, especially when a precise confidence figure has no relation to correctness; incidental learning and informational benefits from social media and communicative AI at work; validity of automated text coding across platforms, models and time.

Measurement

Research

2025
Master's project
Cross-Platform Sentiment Analysis of Public Reaction to UK Economic Policies.
  • Finding. The same policy event produced different sentiment and emotion profiles on different platforms, so a pooled index assumed, without testing, that the classifier measures the same construct everywhere.
  • Study design. Ethics-approved quantitative study (Portsmouth TETHIC-2025-111094) of about 279,000 public posts and comments on UK economic policy from X/Twitter, Reddit, YouTube and Quora, collected through official APIs and licensed feeds under GDPR-compliant procedures. Human-annotated validation set of 200 posts balanced across platforms (reported inter-annotator agreement Cohen's κ ≈ 0.78).
  • Classification and testing. Fine-tuned BERT classifier compared with BiLSTM by McNemar test (p < 0.001) and bootstrap intervals, alongside logistic regression and VADER baselines, with probability calibration. Chi-square tests of sentiment by policy topic; NRC emotion analysis linking anger and fear to negative sentiment in policy discussion.
  • Correction. Re-auditing for the manuscript, I found the headline macro-F1 of 0.878 was agreement with the lexicon-seeded training labels, not with the human set. The manuscript says so and specifies a larger stratified human criterion with a second annotator.
2026
manuscript
Ikram, A. and Haig, E. "Is the Platform Part of the Measurement? A Protocol and Simulation Study for Cross-Platform Equivalence of Machine-Labelled Policy Sentiment." Complete; not published and not submitted.
  • Treats each social platform as a survey mode and asks whether a classifier measures the same construct on each: a generalizability study against a human criterion, invariance and differential item functioning with platform as the grouping facet, and score linking.
  • 500-replication simulation with known ground truth: an index that pools platform scores at face value misstates a known event shift by roughly an eighth and reports the wrong sign of aggregate sentiment on nearly a quarter of days; the measurement-adjusted index gets the sign wrong on about one day in sixty.
No peer-reviewed publications to date.
